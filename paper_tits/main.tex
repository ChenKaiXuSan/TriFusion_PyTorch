\begin{abstract}
Driver monitoring needs reliable 3D body keypoints despite occlusion and changing illumination, yet metric ground truth is impractical to collect inside a vehicle cabin. We present a self-calibrated pipeline that fuses the outputs of an off-the-shelf monocular 3D pose estimator running on three synchronized cabin cameras. First, a triangulation reference built with nominal camera extrinsics suffers session-dependent scale errors of up to six times; we repair it without a calibration target by per-sequence bundle adjustment from the keypoints themselves, with the scale anchored to the estimator's body-size prior, reducing the reprojection error from 10.3 to 3.6 pixels. Second, we propose TriPoseFusion, a network of 28,000 parameters combining masked set attention over any subset of views, corruption-aware view gating, residual refinement, and per-joint uncertainty. Under subject-disjoint cross-validation with one training budget for every method, it lowers the mean per-joint position error from 148.6 to 129.1 mm relative to training-free mean fusion, matching regressors with fifty times more parameters, and when one camera is corrupted by noise it loses about 3\% where those regressors lose 14--15\%. Head yaw from the fused keypoints agrees with an independent head-rotation signal on all 88 drives, even where single cameras fail. On the public Drive\&Act benchmark no model with a learned residual transfers unchanged, but label-free retraining on the new rig, with self-calibrated cameras and supervision only from its own triangulated keypoints, reaches a Procrustes-aligned error of 21.3 mm, on par with the compared methods. We close with three lessons for learning against pseudo references.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{D}{river} monitoring and in-cabin behavior understanding rely on stable 3D representations of the driver's body, which support attention estimation, hand-state analysis, and behavior recognition \cite{abouelnaga2017real,zhang2018driver,gorelik2026multiview}. The cabin is a constrained but difficult setting: the steering wheel, dashboard, and the driver's own arms repeatedly occlude hands and head landmarks, viewpoints are limited by the vehicle layout, and illumination changes sharply between day and night.

Monocular 3D pose estimators \cite{toshev2014deeppose,martinez2017simple,mehta2017vnect,kanazawa2018end} are attractive because they run independently per camera and need no reconstruction infrastructure. Their predictions, however, are depth-ambiguous and view-dependent: each camera returns keypoints in its own root-relative frame, with its own biases, missing joints, and jitter---most severely on the hands and head, where monitoring needs accuracy most. A multi-camera rig observes complementary evidence \cite{iskakov2019learnable,qiu2019cross,tu2020voxelpose}. Exploiting it at the \emph{keypoint level}, the natural interface when the per-view estimator is an off-the-shelf component, raises three coupled problems.

\emph{The reference problem.} Learning and evaluating fusion requires a 3D reference, yet motion capture is impractical in a cabin and checkerboard calibration is hard to repeat whenever a rig is re-mounted. Triangulating 2D keypoints with nominal extrinsics is the common workaround, and it fails silently: on our 22-subject dataset the resulting reference is too large by a per-sequence factor with median $2.4$, ranging from $1.2$ to $6.0$ across sessions (Sec.~\ref{sec:reference}). Every metric error computed against it is dominated by reference scale, and a learned model will absorb that scale and appear to improve (Sec.~\ref{sec:scaleartifact}).

\emph{The fusion problem.} Given well-aligned views, how much does learning improve over averaging, and which component is responsible? Under a protocol in which every method shares inputs, reference, metric code, and training budget, we find that learned view weighting alone adds nothing---and show why its self-supervised variant cannot (Sec.~\ref{sec:collapse})---whereas a reference-supervised residual correction gives consistent gains of up to 13\%, concentrated on the hands. Generic regressors with fifty times more parameters reach the same accuracy, so clean accuracy does not single out an architecture; cost and behavior under failure do.

\emph{The robustness and transfer problem.} A deployed system must tolerate a failed or noisy camera and move to new vehicles. Corruption-aware training lets every learned model survive a dead camera, but only models with explicit per-view weights also survive a noisy one; and residual corrections are rig-specific, so they must be re-learned on a new rig---which our label-free recipe does in minutes.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A self-calibrated triangulation reference} for in-cabin rigs: per-sequence robust bundle adjustment of the extrinsics from the keypoint observations, with the scale gauge anchored to the estimator's body prior. No calibration target and no manual 3D annotation; the reprojection error drops from 10.3 to 3.6\,px, and the physically impossible session-to-session scale of the nominal reference (shoulder widths of 0.46--2.10\,m) is removed.
\item \textbf{\method{}}, a 28K-parameter fusion network with masked set attention over any subset of views, corruption-aware gating, reference-supervised residual refinement, and per-joint Laplace uncertainty. It reduces the error of canonicalized mean fusion by 13.1\%, matches regressors fifty times larger, unlike them tolerates a noisy camera, runs at 0.26\,ms per frame on one CPU thread, and adapts to a new rig without labels.
\item \textbf{A controlled benchmark and three lessons}: fourteen fusion methods under one training budget on 88 in-cabin sequences, a single cross-rig protocol on \da{} \cite{martin2019drive}, an application study validating head-orientation measurement (Sec.~\ref{sec:downstream}), and three failure modes of learning against pseudo references---gate collapse under consensus supervision, illusory gains from reference scale, and illusory architecture differences from unequal training budgets.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Monocular and Video-Based 3D Pose Estimation}
Monocular 3D pose estimation recovers joints by direct regression, 2D-to-3D lifting, or parametric body-model fitting \cite{toshev2014deeppose,martinez2017simple,zhou2017towards,mehta2017vnect,kanazawa2018end}; video models add temporal context \cite{pavllo20193d,kocabas2020vibe}. Such estimators can run on each cabin camera independently, but single-view recovery remains ill-posed under steering-wheel occlusion, truncation, and illumination extremes, producing missing joints, inconsistent scale, and jitter. We treat their outputs as noisy observations to be combined across views.

\subsection{Multi-View Pose Estimation and Keypoint-Level Fusion}
Multi-view methods triangulate 2D detections with calibrated cameras \cite{hartley1997triangulation,iskakov2019learnable}, exchange features or heatmaps across views \cite{qiu2019cross,he2020epipolar}, or aggregate evidence volumetrically \cite{tu2020voxelpose,bultmann2021real,nogueira2024markerless}. They require images and calibrated cameras at inference, and volumetric aggregation is costly on automotive hardware. Our interface is narrower: the fusion stage receives only per-view 3D keypoints, needs no calibration at deployment (calibration serves only to build the training reference), and runs in well under a millisecond per frame. The closest prior work, Chen et al.~\cite{chen2026canonfuse3d}, refines two uncalibrated monocular 3D pose streams of skiers in a body-centric canonical space with cross-view alignment, bidirectional cross-view attention, temporal refinement, and reliability-gated residual fusion, evaluated against synthetic ground truth and, on real video, a fused canonical reference. We share its pose-space interface but differ in three respects: set attention over any subset of the available views instead of a fixed view pair, supervision and evaluation on real data against a self-calibrated triangulation reference, and an explicit study of camera failure and cross-rig transfer. This setting has its own difficulties---mutually inconsistent root-relative frames, missing joints, and time-varying view reliability---and, as we show, it changes which components matter.

\subsection{Pseudo Labels and Their Failure Modes}
Without metric labels, prior work uses reprojection consistency, multi-view agreement, bone-length and temporal priors, or triangulated pseudo labels \cite{rhodin2018learning,kocabas2019self,iskakov2019learnable,wandt2021canonpose,fan2023humanm3}. Cameras can also be calibrated from people themselves: human pose serves as the calibration pattern for unsynchronized sports cameras \cite{takahashi2018human}, person re-identification links wide-baseline views \cite{xu2021wide}, and a single moving person suffices to recover extrinsics \cite{lee2022extrinsic}. Our reference combines both ideas: it refines the extrinsics \emph{per recording session} from human-keypoint correspondences inside a vehicle and anchors the otherwise unobservable scale to the estimator's body prior. Sec.~\ref{sec:lessons} adds three failure modes relevant to this whole line of work.

\subsection{Driver Monitoring Datasets}
In-cabin datasets support distraction and activity recognition \cite{abouelnaga2017real,zhang2018driver,gorelik2026multiview}, head-pose and gaze estimation \cite{roth2019ddpose,selim2020autopose}, and multi-modal attention and alertness monitoring \cite{ortega2020dmd}. \da{} \cite{martin2019drive} additionally provides multi-view recordings with triangulated 3D references, on which 3D driver-body-pose methods have been benchmarked \cite{martin2021evaluation}, and serves as our cross-rig testbed. Our dataset complements it with three RGB cameras, 22 subjects, four driving conditions, and a 52-keypoint protocol that includes both hands.

\section{Self-Calibrated Triangulation Reference}
\label{sec:reference}

\subsection{Rig, Estimator, and Triangulation}
Three cameras (front, left, right) observe the seated driver (Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset}). Each view runs the same monocular estimator, SAM 3D Body~\cite{yang2026sam3dbody}, which outputs 70 2D and 3D keypoints per frame; a 52-keypoint subset (5 head, 5 shoulder/neck, and $2\times21$ hand keypoints) is used throughout. For each joint and frame, every subset of $\geq2$ views with valid observations is triangulated by the direct linear transformation (DLT) \cite{hartley1997triangulation}; the subset with the lowest mean reprojection error is kept, candidates with non-positive depth are rejected, and points whose best error exceeds 40\,px are marked invalid. In practice the kept subset is a pair of views for 99.9\% of the accepted points, so the reference is effectively a best-pair triangulation, and its reprojection error, measured on the two views that define it, is an optimistic quality measure; agreement with an independent reference is assessed on \da{} (Sec.~\ref{sec:driveandact}). Intrinsics were estimated once per camera; the extrinsics, and the metric scale, are the crux.

\subsection{The Nominal-Extrinsics Problem}
\label{sec:nominal}
The extrinsics were originally constructed from the nominal layout of Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset}(b): cameras aimed at the driver from 0.62\,m (front) and 0.85\,m (left and right), with a 0.70\,m left--right baseline. The resulting reference has tolerable reprojection statistics (10.3\,px mean over all observations), which is why its defect went unnoticed. An anatomical audit exposed it: the median triangulated shoulder width over 88 sequences was 0.84\,m and ranged from 0.46 to 2.10\,m depending on the \emph{subject}, i.e., on the recording session (Table~\ref{tab:gtquality}), whereas the estimator's own metric prior was stable at 0.371\,m. The rig had been re-positioned between sessions while one nominal geometry was assumed. Because the error is mostly a scale, it poisons every metric error while remaining nearly invisible to reprojection checks.

\subsection{Per-Sequence Robust Extrinsic Refinement}
\label{sec:persequence}
We refine the extrinsics \emph{per sequence}, with the 2D keypoints as correspondences: the left camera fixes the gauge; the front and right cameras are parameterized as six-degree-of-freedom perturbations of the nominal poses; about 40 evenly spaced frames supply all joints seen in $\geq2$ views; the Huber-robustified reprojection error of DLT-triangulated points is minimized over the 12 camera parameters (structure re-triangulated inside the residual) with a soft prior toward the nominal layout; observations with residuals above 30\,px are rejected and the problem re-solved. The refinement rotates the nominal cameras by a median of 21$^\circ$ (front) and 31$^\circ$ (right), up to 53$^\circ$. Averaged per sequence, the reprojection error drops from 9.6 to 3.3\,px (Table~\ref{tab:gtquality}; pooled over all observations, from 10.3 to 3.6\,px, Table~\ref{tab:gtbreak}), and the front camera takes part in 38\% of the accepted triangulations instead of 8\%; each sequence takes well under a minute on a CPU.

\subsection{Anchoring the Metric Scale Gauge}
\label{sec:anchor}
Rotation refinement cannot fix the scale: with unknown extrinsics, scaling the scene and the camera centers about the fixed camera leaves every reprojection unchanged. Pinning the gauge with the nominal baseline is exactly what failed: the baselines implied by the anchored reconstructions range from 0.25 to 1.07\,m across sessions. We anchor it \emph{anatomically} instead: each reconstruction is rescaled so that its median triangulated shoulder width equals the estimator's median metric shoulder width for that sequence. This costs nothing in reprojection error and makes the reference commensurate with the streams being fused, which is what a fusion error should measure. The resulting shoulder widths ($0.368\pm0.015$\,m) agree with the estimator's prior by construction and are therefore not independent evidence of metric correctness; what the anchor removes is the physically impossible session-to-session scale of the nominal reference. Any global bias of the prior is inherited, scale-invariant metrics do not depend on the anchor, and one tape measurement of a camera baseline would externalize the scale.

Tables~\ref{tab:gtquality} and \ref{tab:gtbreak} summarize the outcome. A single globally refined extrinsics set halves the reprojection error but cannot fix sessions whose geometry differs; its scale spread is even larger. Only the per-sequence procedure with anatomical anchoring is simultaneously low-error (3.6\,px pooled mean, 65\% below nominal), consistent in scale across sessions, balanced across the four driving conditions, and balanced across views: under nominal extrinsics the front camera contributed only 1.4M usable observations, because its wrong pose pushed its errors past the acceptance threshold; after self-calibration it contributes 6.6M ($4.7\times$) at the lowest median error of the three views. All experiments use this reference.

\textbf{Check on a calibrated rig.} We ran the recipe unchanged (pixel constants scaled by the focal-length ratio) on the six \da{} runs of Sec.~\ref{sec:driveandact}, starting the front and right cameras from the official extrinsics perturbed by up to $45^\circ$ and 20\,cm (13 starts per run). Up to $30^\circ$/15\,cm every start converges to the same rig, $4.5^\circ$ (median; at most $5.8^\circ$) and 52\,mm from the official one, yet with lower held-out reprojection error (4.1 vs.\ 4.8\,px); from $45^\circ$ the worst start ends $8.7^\circ$ off. Against the official 3D annotation (14 joints, body-centered frame), the resulting reference has 63.3\,mm MPJPE and 33.0\,mm PA-MPJPE, against 48.8 and 29.3\,mm for three-view triangulation with the official calibration. The self-calibrated extrinsics cost 0.6\,mm of this gap (62.7\,mm with the official extrinsics, same anchor and view selection). The rest comes from the scale anchor (7.6\,mm; the estimator's shoulder width exceeds the triangulated one by 6\% on average, $-2$ to $+14\%$ per run) and from keeping the best pair of views, which the rule does for every point (6.3\,mm). Errors measured against our reference therefore include reference error of this size, and its absolute scale carries the estimator's bias. Retraining on \da{} with these extrinsics instead of the official ones changes PA-MPJPE by at most 1.4\,mm (Sec.~\ref{sec:driveandact}).

\subsection{Problem Setting}
At inference, \method{} receives synchronized monocular 3D keypoint streams $P_v \in \mathbb{R}^{T\times J\times 3}$ from the available views $v \in \mathcal{V}$, $1 \le |\mathcal{V}| \le 3$, $J{=}52$, and outputs a fused sequence and a per-joint uncertainty (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). No calibration and no images are needed at inference; the reference of Sec.~\ref{sec:reference} is used only for training.

\subsection{Robust Body-Centered Canonicalization}
\label{sec:canon}
Each view is mapped into a body-centered frame, adapting the canonicalization of \cite{chen2026canonfuse3d} to a seated driver: the neck and shoulders take the place of the pelvis and hips, which are hidden in the cabin. With neck $o_t$ and shoulders $s^l_t, s^r_t$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_t = \frac{s^r_t - s^l_t}{\|s^r_t - s^l_t\|},\quad
\mathbf{d}_t = \tfrac{1}{2}(s^l_t+s^r_t) - o_t,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_t = \frac{\mathbf{x}_t \times \mathbf{d}_t}{\|\mathbf{x}_t \times \mathbf{d}_t\|},\quad
\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{z}_t \times \mathbf{x}_t,\quad
\bar{P}^{t,j}_v = R_t^\top (P^{t,j}_v - o_t),
\end{equation}
with $R_t = [\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{y}_t, \mathbf{z}_t]$, computed for each view separately (view index omitted). If a frame's shoulders are degenerate (coincident, or an implausible width), the shoulder axis of the temporally nearest valid frame is reused---substituting a zero vector collapses $R_t$ and maps the whole frame to the origin---and a fixed reference axis restores full rank when $\mathbf{x}_t$ and $\mathbf{d}_t$ become near-parallel. Canonicalization makes the streams commensurable: it reduces cross-view disagreement from the 2\,m scale of root-relative camera frames to the 0.15\,m scale.

\subsection{Masked Set Attention over Views}
For each joint $j$ and frame $t$, the canonicalized observations of the available views form a token set,
\begin{equation}
u^{t,j}_v = W_{\mathrm{in}}\big[\bar{P}^{t,j}_v;\; \delta^{t,j}_v;\; m^{t,j}_v\big] + e^{\mathrm{view}}_v + e^{\mathrm{joint}}_j,
\end{equation}
where $\delta^{t,j}_v$ is the distance to the mean of the valid views (a consistency cue), $m^{t,j}_v$ a validity flag, and $e^{\mathrm{view}}, e^{\mathrm{joint}}$ learned embeddings. Two transformer layers ($d{=}32$, 4 heads) attend \emph{across the view set} with invalid views masked. Set attention is permutation-equivariant \cite{zaheer2017deep}, so one network accepts any subset of cameras, and a view that drops out at runtime is treated exactly like one that was never installed. A linear head maps each token to a gate logit, and a masked softmax over the set $\mathcal{V}_{t,j}$ of views valid for joint $j$ at frame $t$ gives per-joint, per-frame weights and the gated average
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{t,j,v} = \frac{\exp g^{t,j}_v}{\sum_{v'\in\mathcal{V}_{t,j}} \exp g^{t,j}_{v'}},\qquad
\hat{p}_{t,j} = \sum_{v} \alpha_{t,j,v}\, \bar{P}^{t,j}_v .
\end{equation}
The gate-pooled feature passes one transformer layer across the $J$ joints (skeleton context, e.g., wrist evidence informing finger joints) and a depthwise temporal convolution (kernel 5); two zero-initialized heads then output a residual and a Laplace log-scale $s_{t,j}$:
\begin{equation}
p^{\mathrm{out}}_{t,j} = \hat{p}_{t,j} + \Delta_{t,j},\qquad \sigma_{t,j} = \exp(s_{t,j}).
\end{equation}
The residual can move a joint \emph{away} from all observations---for example, to correct a depth bias shared by every view---which convex weighting cannot do.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:training}
The only loss is a masked Laplace negative log-likelihood (NLL) against the self-calibrated reference $g_{t,j}$ over its valid joints $\Omega$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{(t,j)\in\Omega} \Big( \|p^{\mathrm{out}}_{t,j}-g_{t,j}\|_1 \, e^{-s_{t,j}} + 3\, s_{t,j} \Big),
\label{eq:nll}
\end{equation}
where the factor 3 reflects the three coordinates sharing one scale; the loss trains the residual and calibrates the uncertainty jointly \cite{kendall2017uncertainties}. With probability 0.5 per training sample, one random view is corrupted: dropped, zeroed (a constant stream, i.e., an undetected sensor failure), or perturbed with 100\,mm Gaussian noise. This \emph{corruption augmentation} is what teaches the gate to recognize unreliable views; without an external signal and corrupted examples the gate has no reason to encode reliability (Sec.~\ref{sec:collapse}). Training uses Adam ($10^{-3}$), 6{,}000 steps of 32 windows of 64 frames (about 23 passes over the training frames), a fixed seed (the four key models are repeated with two more seeds), and the final checkpoint (no selection on the evaluation fold); one fold takes about 13 minutes on one GPU. We deliberately use no consensus self-supervision, no contrastive view-alignment loss (which erases the inter-view differences the gate must read), and no extra capacity (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\textbf{In-cabin dataset} (Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset}). 22 subjects and four driving conditions---day or night, each with a busy or a quiet simulated driving environment (many or few elements in the scene)---with one sequence per subject and condition: 88 sequences, $\sim$0.7M frames per camera, $\sim$17M valid reference joint observations. The keypoint streams cover the first 77\% of each drive on average (Sec.~\ref{sec:downstream}).

\textbf{\da{}} \cite{martin2019drive}. Official split-0 test subjects (vp5, vp11, vp13; two runs each) and three near-infrared views (inner mirror and both A-pillars). Training-free evaluation uses 7{,}855 triplets sampled at 1\,fps; model experiments use dense 30\,fps clips (three 20\,s segments per run; 10{,}799 frames). The reference is the official multi-view triangulation of OpenPose \cite{cao2021openpose} detections; knee and ankle confidences are zero in the cabin, so models are evaluated on the nine joints shared with our keypoint set (head, shoulders, wrists).

\textbf{Protocol.} Subject-disjoint 5-fold cross-validation in-cabin and leave-one-subject-out (3 folds) on \da{}; every sequence is scored, as a whole, by a model that never saw its subject. All learned methods share one training budget (Sec.~\ref{sec:training}), the same corruption augmentation, and the final checkpoint, and all methods consume identical cached inputs, reference, validity masks, and metric code---unequal budgets or mixed canonicalization implementations alone can fabricate differences as large as those between architectures (Sec.~\ref{sec:lessons}).

\textbf{Metrics} (all in mm). Prediction and reference are compared in the body-centered frame: the reference is canonicalized with its own neck and shoulders exactly like the inputs (Sec.~\ref{sec:canon}), so errors exclude global position and orientation, and neck and shoulder errors are small by construction. The mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) is the mean Euclidean error over all valid joint instances of a sequence (frames with more visible hand joints weigh more), averaged over sequences and folds. PA-MPJPE applies the least-squares similarity alignment \cite{umeyama1991least} on the same joints. The percentage of correct keypoints PCK@100 is the fraction of joints within 100\,mm. Fold-to-fold standard deviations (22--32\,mm) reflect subject difficulty and are shared by all methods, so differences between methods are tested per sequence: a paired difference is the mean of the 88 per-sequence differences (hence not the difference of fold means), with a 95\% interval from 10{,}000 subject-level bootstrap resamples. Each model is trained once per fold with a fixed seed.

\textbf{Baselines.} \emph{Training-free}: single views; the per-sequence oracle best view (a diagnostic bound, not deployable); canonicalized mean and median fusion; the geometric median over views \cite{weiszfeld1937point}; leave-one-out consensus, discarding a view that deviates by more than 100\,mm and twice the others' spread \cite{fischler1981random}; mean fusion with temporal Gaussian smoothing; and Procrustes re-alignment of the views before averaging \cite{gower1975generalized}. \emph{Learned}, all trained on the same reference with the same augmentation, as zero-initialized residuals on mean fusion: a per-joint MLP on the concatenated views (Concat-MLP, 6.1K parameters); a whole-skeleton lifting MLP after \cite{martinez2017simple} (1.46M); a dilated temporal CNN after \cite{pavllo20193d} with a 29-frame receptive field (1.31M); a transformer over joints after the spatial encoder of \cite{zheng2021poseformer} (105K); convex view weighting (1.9K), the keypoint-level analogue of learned-confidence triangulation \cite{iskakov2019learnable}; a residual-only model whose 8K-parameter head reads the raw per-view coordinates behind an independent per-view gate; and our earlier design (v1: per-view encoders, cross-view attention, gate, dilated temporal network; hidden width 64, 261K), retrained with our supervision. Image-based multi-view estimators \cite{iskakov2019learnable,tu2020voxelpose} need images and calibrated cameras at inference and cannot run on keypoint inputs; triangulation of the same 2D keypoints is the reference itself on our rig and is evaluated only on \da{}.

\section{Application: Head-Orientation Measurement}
\label{sec:downstream}
Pose accuracy matters for driver monitoring only if it yields valid behavioral signals. We test this for head yaw---the quantity behind mirror checks, glance behavior, and attention estimation---on all 88 in-cabin drives, with identical measurement code for every keypoint source. The aim is to validate the measurement, not to draw behavioral conclusions.

\subsection{Measurement Protocol}
Head yaw is the horizontal angle of the vector from the ear midpoint to the nose in the body frame of Sec.~\ref{sec:canon}, i.e., head rotation relative to the torso; it is smoothed over 0.3\,s and referenced to its median over the drive. A head turn is a $|\mathrm{yaw}|>15^\circ$ excursion lasting at least 0.2\,s. Two references are used. The \emph{independent signal} is the estimator's head rotation matrix, a separate output head that does not depend on nose or ear visibility, brought into the cabin frame with the self-calibrated extrinsics and averaged over the views. The \emph{human reference} is the left/right glance annotation of three raters, an event being kept when at least two agree (1{,}692 events; frame-wise Fleiss $\kappa=0.72$). The annotation tool counts frames at 24\,fps against 29.97\,fps video, so annotated frame indices are rescaled by 1.249 before any comparison; without this step the two time bases drift apart by 52--104\,s (median 69\,s) by the end of a drive. The keypoint streams cover on average the first 77\% of each annotated drive, and all statistics use the covered part. The two yaw signals differ in frame: keypoint yaw is head rotation relative to the torso, the check signal relative to the cabin. The torso turns little (median spread 2.5--5.1$^\circ$ per drive, depending on how it is estimated) but follows large head turns (slope 0.14--0.24 for check angles above $20^\circ$). Subtracting it does not yield a cleaner reference: the estimator's own shoulder axis shares its errors with the keypoints' body frame (mean fusion then reaches $r=0.99$), and the triangulated shoulder axis is too noisy ($r$ falls to 0.61--0.71 for every source). We therefore keep the cabin-frame check and state which conclusions hold under all three references.

\subsection{Agreement with Human Annotations and Stability Across Conditions}
The sign of keypoint yaw during annotated left/right glances agrees with the labeled direction for 96\% of the events with fused keypoints and 95\% with single views, and 93\% of the head turns detected from \method{} overlap an annotated glance. With one-to-one matching of turns to glances, precision is similar for all sources (0.52--0.58), and recall is highest for \method{} (0.44 vs.\ 0.36--0.39; F1 0.49 vs.\ 0.43--0.46). Fewer than half of the annotated glances thus come with a head turn of at least $15^\circ$; the rest are eye-dominant, so glance annotations and head turns are related but distinct measures. The measurement is stable across driving conditions: with \method{}, the head-turn rate is 2.79, 2.73, 2.48, and 2.61 per minute in the day--busy, day--quiet, night--busy, and night--quiet conditions (Friedman test over the 22 subjects, $p=0.82$), and neither the peak angle ($p=0.89$) nor the yaw spread ($p=0.26$) differs between conditions.

\textbf{Scope.} We validate yaw only: head pitch correlates weakly with the reference pitch for every source (0.32--0.45; 0.40 for \method{}). The independent signal, being a separate output of the same estimator, is only partially independent. And the last quarter of each drive is not yet covered by keypoints.

\section{Lessons for Learning Against Pseudo References}
\label{sec:lessons}

\subsection{Consensus Self-Supervision Collapses View Gating}
\label{sec:collapse}
Our earlier design (v1) was trained against the per-frame coordinate-wise median of its own canonicalized inputs, with view-consistency, bone-length, temporal-smoothness, and contrastive view-alignment terms. Consider any fusion $\hat{p} = \sum_v \alpha_v \bar{P}_v$ with $\alpha$ in the simplex, trained to minimize $\mathbb{E}\,\rho(\hat{p} - T(\bar{P}_{1:V}))$ where the teacher $T$ is a symmetric central-tendency statistic (mean or median) of the same inputs. For the mean teacher, the uniform weight $\alpha_v = 1/V$ reproduces the teacher exactly and is the unique minimizer whenever the views disagree, so the gate receives no signal to depart from averaging, whatever the architecture. For the coordinate-wise median teacher an input-dependent gate could do better by selecting the middle observation, but such a gate only re-implements a robust statistic of the current inputs; it still has no access to which view is reliable on a given rig. Empirically, every v1 configuration we trained this way---with or without cross-view attention, under different regularization weights, and with a leave-one-view-out teacher---converged to gates of $0.333/0.333/0.333$, scoring 185.7\,mm against 185.8\,mm for mean fusion on its evaluation fold (fold 0, under the nominal-extrinsics reference), and the collapse reproduced on \da{}. Adaptive weighting needs information \emph{external} to the views being weighted: here, the triangulation reference and corruption augmentation.

\subsection{Reference-Scale Errors Manufacture Illusory Gains}
\label{sec:scaleartifact}
Against the nominal-extrinsics reference, a 1.9K-parameter fusion model with a temporal head appeared to beat mean fusion by 12.5\%, consistently across folds. The reference is too large by a median factor of 2.4, and the temporal convolution had learned an approximately global inflation of its output (output scale $2.1\times$ the input), which reduces error against a mis-scaled reference while corrupting the pose. The signatures: the gain vanished without the temporal head; scale-sensitive and scale-invariant metrics moved in opposite directions; and zero-shot transfer to \da{}, whose reference has correct scale, \emph{degraded} PA-MPJPE from 24.3 to 36.2\,mm (31.5--41.4\,mm over the five fold models) and MPJPE from 24.7 to 317\,mm under the protocol of Table~\ref{tab:da}---the signature of an inflated output. Trained against the anchored reference, the same model equals mean fusion (148.6 vs.\ 148.6\,mm). A scale-consistent reference, or at least a scale-invariant metric, is a prerequisite for claiming learned improvements.

\subsection{Unequal Training Budgets Manufacture Architecture Differences}
Before equalizing budgets, the residual-only model (trained on 6{,}000 steps of 32 $\times$ 64 frames) appeared to beat the full model (3{,}000 steps of 16 $\times$ 32 frames, eight times fewer frames) by 14.3\% against 11.8\% over mean fusion. With one budget they are indistinguishable (128.9 vs.\ 129.1\,mm). Comparisons of fusion architectures are meaningful only at a common, reported budget.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
On a healthy three-camera rig, canonicalized averaging is nearly optimal among methods that only weight views, and a gain of up to 13\% is available to \emph{any} model that adds a reference-supervised residual, including generic regressors fifty times larger. The case for a small gated model is therefore not peak accuracy but what comes with it: tolerance to a noisy camera, inspectable view weights, calibrated per-joint confidence, insensitivity to the adaptation budget on a new rig, and a cost of 28K parameters. Together with a reference that can be rebuilt on any rig without a target, this makes the pipeline deployable: calibrate from the driver's own keypoints, retrain without labels, and run on a CPU.

\textbf{Limitations.} On Procrustes-aligned error the model trails whole-skeleton regressors by 2.6\,mm; combining view gating with a whole-skeleton residual is a natural next design, with the caveat that raw-coordinate residuals transferred worst. No learned residual model transfers zero-shot, so a new vehicle requires the label-free adaptation. The in-cabin reference is triangulated from the same 2D detections that feed the fused streams; \da{}'s independent reference partially fills this gap, but a physically independent sensor would strengthen absolute claims. Hands dominate the error budget (202\,mm vs.\ 48\,mm for non-hand joints) and are at times invisible to all cameras. Apart from the four key models, which were trained with three seeds, each model was trained once per fold. The gate only partially detects Gaussian corruption; head pitch is not validated; the scale anchor inherits the global bias of the estimator's body prior (6\% on \da{}); and the data come from a driving simulator, so validation on moving vehicles with vibration remains future work.
