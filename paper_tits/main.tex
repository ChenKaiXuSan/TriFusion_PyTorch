\begin{abstract}
Driver monitoring needs reliable 3D body keypoints despite occlusion and changing illumination, yet metric ground truth is impractical to collect inside a vehicle cabin. We present a self-calibrated pipeline that fuses the outputs of an off-the-shelf monocular 3D pose estimator running on three synchronized cabin cameras. First, a triangulation reference built with nominal camera extrinsics suffers session-dependent scale errors of up to six times; we repair it without a calibration target by per-sequence bundle adjustment from the keypoints themselves, with the scale anchored to the estimator's body-size prior, reducing the reprojection error from 10.3 to 3.6 pixels. Second, we propose TriPoseFusion v2, a 28K-parameter network combining masked set attention over any subset of views, corruption-aware view gating, residual refinement, and per-joint uncertainty. Under subject-disjoint cross-validation with one training budget for every method, it lowers the mean per-joint position error from 148.6 to 129.1 mm relative to the best training-free fusion, matching regressors with fifty times more parameters, and when one camera is corrupted by noise it loses about 3\% where those regressors lose 14--15\%. Head yaw derived from the fused keypoints agrees with an independent head-rotation signal on all 88 drives and reproduces head-turn amplitude. On the public Drive\&Act benchmark no learned model transfers unchanged, but label-free retraining on the new rig, supervised only by its own triangulated keypoints, reaches a Procrustes-aligned error of 20.8 mm, the best or tied-best among all compared methods. We close with three lessons for learning against pseudo references.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{D}{river} monitoring and in-cabin behavior understanding rely on stable 3D representations of the driver's body, which support attention estimation, hand-state analysis, and behavior recognition \cite{abouelnaga2017real,zhang2018driver,gorelik2026multiview}. The cabin is a constrained but difficult setting: the steering wheel, dashboard, and the driver's own arms repeatedly occlude hands and head landmarks, viewpoints are limited by the vehicle layout, and illumination changes sharply between day and night.

Monocular 3D pose estimators \cite{toshev2014deeppose,martinez2017simple,mehta2017vnect,kanazawa2018end} are attractive because they run independently per camera and need no reconstruction infrastructure. Their predictions, however, are depth-ambiguous and view-dependent: each camera returns keypoints in its own root-relative frame, with its own biases, missing joints, and jitter---most severely on the hands and head, where monitoring needs accuracy most. A multi-camera rig observes complementary evidence \cite{iskakov2019learnable,qiu2019cross,tu2020voxelpose}. Exploiting it at the \emph{keypoint level}, the natural interface when the per-view estimator is an off-the-shelf component, raises three coupled problems.

\emph{The reference problem.} Learning and evaluating fusion requires a 3D reference, yet motion capture is impractical in a cabin and checkerboard calibration is hard to repeat whenever a rig is re-mounted. Triangulating 2D keypoints with nominal extrinsics is the common workaround, and it fails silently: on our 22-subject dataset the resulting reference is too large by a median factor of $2.4$, varying by session from $1.2$ to $6$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:reference}). Every metric error computed against it is dominated by reference scale, and a learned model will absorb that scale and appear to improve (Sec.~\ref{sec:scaleartifact}).

\emph{The fusion problem.} Given well-aligned views, how much does learning improve over averaging, and which component is responsible? Under a protocol in which every method shares inputs, reference, metric code, and training budget, we find that learned view weighting alone adds nothing---and prove why its self-supervised variant cannot (Sec.~\ref{sec:collapse})---whereas a reference-supervised residual correction gives consistent gains of up to 13\%, concentrated on the hands. Generic regressors with fifty times more parameters reach the same accuracy, so clean accuracy does not single out an architecture; cost and behavior under failure do.

\emph{The robustness and transfer problem.} A deployed system must tolerate a failed or noisy camera and move to new vehicles. Corruption-aware training lets every learned model survive a dead camera, but only models with explicit per-view weights also survive a noisy one; and residual corrections are rig-specific, so they must be re-learned on a new rig---which our label-free recipe does in minutes.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A self-calibrated triangulation reference} for in-cabin rigs: per-sequence robust bundle adjustment of the extrinsics from the keypoint observations, with the scale gauge anchored to the estimator's body prior. No calibration target and no manual 3D annotation; the reprojection error drops from 10.3 to 3.6\,px and the shoulder-width spread across sessions from 0.37\,m to 0.015\,m.
\item \textbf{\method{}}, a 28K-parameter fusion network with masked set attention over any subset of views, corruption-aware gating, reference-supervised residual refinement, and per-joint Laplace uncertainty. It reduces the error of the best training-free fusion by 13.1\%, matches regressors fifty times larger, unlike them tolerates a noisy camera, runs in under 0.6\,ms per frame on one CPU thread, and adapts to a new rig without labels.
\item \textbf{A controlled benchmark and three lessons}: fourteen fusion methods under one training budget on 88 in-cabin sequences, a single cross-rig protocol on \da{} \cite{martin2019drive}, an application study validating head-orientation measurement (Sec.~\ref{sec:downstream}), and three failure modes of learning against pseudo references---gate collapse under consensus supervision, illusory gains from reference scale, and illusory architecture differences from unequal training budgets.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Monocular and Video-Based 3D Pose Estimation}
Monocular 3D pose estimation recovers joints by direct regression, 2D-to-3D lifting, or parametric body-model fitting \cite{toshev2014deeppose,martinez2017simple,zhou2017towards,mehta2017vnect,kanazawa2018end}; video models add temporal context \cite{pavllo20193d,kocabas2020vibe}. Such estimators can run on each cabin camera independently, but single-view recovery remains ill-posed under steering-wheel occlusion, truncation, and illumination extremes, producing missing joints, inconsistent scale, and jitter. We treat their outputs as noisy observations to be combined across views.

\subsection{Multi-View Pose Estimation and Keypoint-Level Fusion}
Multi-view methods triangulate 2D detections with calibrated cameras \cite{hartley1997triangulation,iskakov2019learnable}, exchange features or heatmaps across views \cite{qiu2019cross,he2020epipolar}, or aggregate evidence volumetrically \cite{tu2020voxelpose,bultmann2021real,nogueira2024markerless}. They require images and calibrated cameras at inference, and volumetric aggregation is costly on automotive hardware. Our interface is narrower: the fusion stage receives only per-view 3D keypoints, needs no calibration at deployment (calibration serves only to build the training reference), and runs in microseconds. The closest prior work, Chen et al.~\cite{chen2026canonfuse3d}, refines two uncalibrated monocular 3D pose streams of skiers in a body-centric canonical space with cross-view alignment, bidirectional cross-view attention, temporal refinement, and reliability-gated residual fusion, evaluated against synthetic ground truth and, on real video, a fused canonical reference. We share its pose-space interface but differ in three respects: set attention over any subset of the available views instead of a fixed view pair, supervision and evaluation on real data against a self-calibrated triangulation reference, and an explicit study of camera failure and cross-rig transfer. This setting has its own difficulties---mutually inconsistent root-relative frames, missing joints, and time-varying view reliability---and, as we show, it changes which components matter.

\subsection{Pseudo Labels and Their Failure Modes}
Without metric labels, prior work uses reprojection consistency, multi-view agreement, bone-length and temporal priors, or triangulated pseudo labels \cite{rhodin2018learning,kocabas2019self,iskakov2019learnable,wandt2021canonpose,fan2023humanm3}. Our reference belongs to this family but refines the extrinsics \emph{per recording session} from human-keypoint correspondences and anchors the otherwise unobservable scale to the estimator's body prior. Sec.~\ref{sec:lessons} adds three failure modes relevant to this whole line of work.

\subsection{Driver Monitoring Datasets}
In-cabin datasets support distraction and activity recognition \cite{abouelnaga2017real,zhang2018driver,gorelik2026multiview}; \da{} \cite{martin2019drive} additionally provides multi-view recordings with triangulated 3D references and serves as our cross-rig testbed. Our dataset complements it with three RGB cameras, 22 subjects, four illumination settings, and a 52-keypoint protocol that includes both hands.

\section{Self-Calibrated Triangulation Reference}
\label{sec:reference}

\subsection{Rig, Estimator, and Triangulation}
Three cameras (front, left, right) observe the seated driver (Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset}). Each view runs the same monocular estimator (SAM3D-Body), which outputs 70 2D and 3D keypoints per frame; a 52-keypoint subset (5 head, 5 shoulder/neck, and $2\times21$ hand keypoints) is used throughout. For each joint and frame, every subset of $\geq2$ views with valid observations is triangulated by DLT \cite{hartley1997triangulation}; the subset with the lowest mean reprojection error is kept, candidates with non-positive depth are rejected, and points whose best error exceeds 40\,px are marked invalid. Per-point quality statistics are stored with the reference. Intrinsics were estimated once per camera; the extrinsics, and the metric scale, are the crux.

\subsection{The Nominal-Extrinsics Problem}
\label{sec:nominal}
The extrinsics were originally constructed from a nominal layout (look-at poses from measured target distances and a 0.70\,m left--right baseline). The resulting reference has tolerable reprojection statistics (10.3\,px mean), which is why its defect went unnoticed. An anatomical audit exposed it: the median triangulated shoulder width over 88 sequences was 0.84\,m and ranged from 0.46 to 2.10\,m depending on the \emph{subject}, i.e., on the recording session (Table~\ref{tab:gtquality}), whereas the estimator's own metric prior was stable at 0.371\,m. The rig had been re-positioned between sessions while one nominal geometry was assumed. Because the error is mostly a scale, it poisons every metric error while remaining nearly invisible to reprojection checks.

\subsection{Per-Sequence Robust Extrinsic Refinement}
\label{sec:persequence}
We refine the extrinsics \emph{per sequence}, with the 2D keypoints as correspondences: the left camera fixes the gauge; the front and right cameras are parameterized as 6-DoF perturbations of the nominal poses; about 40 evenly spaced frames supply all joints seen in $\geq2$ views; the Huber-robustified reprojection error of DLT-triangulated points is minimized over the 12 camera parameters (structure re-triangulated inside the residual) with a soft prior toward the nominal layout; observations with residuals above 30\,px are rejected and the problem re-solved. The refinement rotates the nominal cameras by a median of 21$^\circ$ (front) and 31$^\circ$ (right), up to 53$^\circ$. On held-out frames the mean reprojection error drops from 9.6 to 3.4\,px, and the front camera's share of accepted triangulations rises from 8\% to 45\%; each sequence takes well under a minute on a CPU.

\subsection{Anchoring the Metric Scale Gauge}
\label{sec:anchor}
Rotation refinement cannot fix the scale: with unknown extrinsics, scaling the scene and the camera centers about the fixed camera leaves every reprojection unchanged. Pinning the gauge with the nominal baseline is exactly what failed---back-solved from the refined geometry, the true baselines ranged from 0.25 to 1.07\,m. We anchor it \emph{anatomically} instead: each reconstruction is rescaled so that its median triangulated shoulder width equals the estimator's median metric shoulder width for that sequence. This costs nothing in reprojection error, makes the reference commensurate with the streams being fused, and is validated by convergence: shoulder widths concentrate at $0.368\pm0.015$\,m over all 88 sequences, in agreement with the estimator prior and with anatomy. Any global bias of the prior is inherited; scale-invariant metrics are unaffected, and one tape measurement of a baseline would externalize the scale.

\subsection{Problem Setting}
At inference, \method{} receives synchronized monocular 3D keypoint streams $P_v \in \mathbb{R}^{T\times J\times 3}$ from the available views $v \in \mathcal{V}$, $1 \le |\mathcal{V}| \le 3$, $J{=}52$, and outputs a fused sequence and a per-joint uncertainty (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). No calibration and no images are needed at inference; the reference of Sec.~\ref{sec:reference} is used only for training.

\subsection{Robust Body-Centred Canonicalization}
\label{sec:canon}
Each view is mapped into a body-centred frame, adapting the canonicalization of \cite{chen2026canonfuse3d} to a seated driver: the neck and shoulders take the place of the pelvis and hips, which are hidden in the cabin. With neck $o_t$ and shoulders $s^l_t, s^r_t$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_t = \frac{s^r_t - s^l_t}{\|s^r_t - s^l_t\|},\quad
\mathbf{d}_t = \tfrac{1}{2}(s^l_t+s^r_t) - o_t,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_t = \frac{\mathbf{x}_t \times \mathbf{d}_t}{\|\mathbf{x}_t \times \mathbf{d}_t\|},\quad
\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{z}_t \times \mathbf{x}_t,\quad
\bar{P}^{t,j}_v = R_t^\top (P^{t,j}_v - o_t),
\end{equation}
with $R_t = [\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{y}_t, \mathbf{z}_t]$. If a frame's shoulders are degenerate (coincident, or an implausible width), the shoulder axis of the temporally nearest valid frame is reused---substituting a zero vector collapses $R_t$ and maps the whole frame to the origin---and a fixed reference axis restores full rank when $\mathbf{x}_t$ and $\mathbf{d}_t$ become near-parallel. Canonicalization makes the streams commensurable: it reduces cross-view disagreement from the 2\,m scale of root-relative camera frames to the 0.15\,m scale.

\subsection{Masked Set Attention over Views}
For each joint $j$ and frame $t$, the canonicalized observations of the available views form a token set,
\begin{equation}
u^{t,j}_v = W_{\mathrm{in}}\big[\bar{P}^{t,j}_v;\; \delta^{t,j}_v;\; m^{t,j}_v\big] + e^{\mathrm{view}}_v + e^{\mathrm{joint}}_j,
\end{equation}
where $\delta^{t,j}_v$ is the distance to the mean of the valid views (a consistency cue), $m^{t,j}_v$ a validity flag, and $e^{\mathrm{view}}, e^{\mathrm{joint}}$ learned embeddings. Two transformer layers ($d{=}32$, 4 heads) attend \emph{across the view set} with invalid views masked. Set attention is permutation-equivariant \cite{zaheer2017deep}, so one network accepts any subset of cameras, and a view that drops out at runtime is treated exactly like one that was never installed. A linear head maps each token to a gate logit, and a masked softmax gives per-joint, per-frame weights and the gated average
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{t,j,v} = \frac{\exp g^{t,j}_v}{\sum_{v'\in\mathcal{V}_{t,j}} \exp g^{t,j}_{v'}},\qquad
\hat{p}_{t,j} = \sum_{v} \alpha_{t,j,v}\, \bar{P}^{t,j}_v .
\end{equation}
The gate-pooled feature passes one transformer layer across the $J$ joints (skeleton context, e.g., wrist evidence informing finger joints) and a depthwise temporal convolution (kernel 5); two zero-initialized heads then output a residual and a Laplace log-scale $s_{t,j}$:
\begin{equation}
p^{\mathrm{out}}_{t,j} = \hat{p}_{t,j} + \Delta_{t,j},\qquad \sigma_{t,j} = \exp(s_{t,j}).
\end{equation}
The residual can move a joint \emph{away} from all observations---for example, to correct a depth bias shared by every view---which convex weighting cannot do.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:training}
The only loss is a masked Laplace negative log-likelihood against the self-calibrated reference $g_{t,j}$ over its valid joints $\Omega$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{(t,j)\in\Omega} \Big( \|p^{\mathrm{out}}_{t,j}-g_{t,j}\|_1 \, e^{-s_{t,j}} + 3\, s_{t,j} \Big),
\label{eq:nll}
\end{equation}
which trains the residual and calibrates the uncertainty jointly \cite{kendall2017uncertainties}. With probability 0.5 per training sample, one random view is corrupted: dropped, zeroed (a constant stream, i.e., an undetected sensor failure), or perturbed with 100\,mm Gaussian noise. This \emph{corruption augmentation} is what teaches the gate to recognize unreliable views; without an external signal and corrupted examples the gate has no reason to encode reliability (Sec.~\ref{sec:collapse}). Training uses Adam ($10^{-3}$), 6{,}000 steps of 32 windows of 64 frames (about 23 passes over the training frames), a fixed seed, and the final checkpoint (no selection on the evaluation fold); one fold takes about 13 minutes on one GPU. We deliberately use no consensus self-supervision, no contrastive view-alignment loss (which erases the inter-view differences the gate must read), and no extra capacity (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\textbf{In-cabin dataset} (Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset}). 22 subjects, four illumination settings (day/night $\times$ high/low), one sequence per subject and setting: 88 sequences, $\sim$0.7M frames per camera, $\sim$17M valid reference joint observations.

\textbf{\da{}} \cite{martin2019drive}. Official split-0 test subjects (vp5, vp11, vp13; two runs each) and three near-infrared views (inner mirror and both A-pillars). Training-free evaluation uses 7{,}855 triplets sampled at 1\,fps; model experiments use dense 30\,fps clips (three 20\,s segments per run; 10{,}799 frames). The reference is the official multi-view OpenPose triangulation; knee and ankle confidences are zero in the cabin, so models are evaluated on the nine joints shared with our keypoint set (head, shoulders, wrists).

\textbf{Protocol.} Subject-disjoint 5-fold cross-validation in-cabin and leave-one-subject-out (3 folds) on \da{}; every sequence is scored, as a whole, by a model that never saw its subject. All learned methods share one training budget (Sec.~\ref{sec:training}), the same corruption augmentation, and the final checkpoint, and all methods consume identical cached inputs, reference, validity masks, and metric code---unequal budgets or mixed canonicalization implementations alone can fabricate differences as large as those between architectures (Sec.~\ref{sec:lessons}).

\textbf{Metrics} (all in mm). MPJPE is the mean error over all valid joint instances of a sequence (frames with more visible hand joints weigh more), averaged over sequences and folds. PA-MPJPE applies the least-squares similarity alignment \cite{umeyama1991least} on the same joints. PCK@100 is the fraction of joints within 100\,mm. Fold-to-fold standard deviations (24--32\,mm) reflect subject difficulty and are shared by all methods, so differences between methods are tested per sequence with a subject-level bootstrap.

\textbf{Baselines.} \emph{Training-free}: single views; the per-sequence oracle best view (a diagnostic bound, not deployable); canonicalized mean and median fusion; the geometric median over views \cite{weiszfeld1937point}; leave-one-out consensus, discarding a view that deviates by more than 100\,mm and twice the others' spread \cite{fischler1981random}; mean fusion with temporal Gaussian smoothing; and Procrustes re-alignment of the views before averaging \cite{gower1975generalized}. \emph{Learned}, all trained on the same reference with the same augmentation, as zero-initialized residuals on mean fusion: a per-joint MLP on the concatenated views (Concat-MLP, 6.1K parameters); a whole-skeleton lifting MLP after \cite{martinez2017simple} (1.46M); a dilated temporal CNN after \cite{pavllo20193d} with a 29-frame receptive field (1.31M); a transformer over joints after the spatial encoder of \cite{zheng2021poseformer} (105K); convex view weighting (1.9K), the keypoint-level analogue of learned-confidence triangulation \cite{iskakov2019learnable}; a residual-only model whose 8K-parameter head reads the raw per-view coordinates behind an independent per-view gate; and our earlier design (v1: per-view encoders, cross-view attention, gate, dilated temporal network; 261K), retrained with our supervision. Image-based multi-view estimators \cite{iskakov2019learnable,tu2020voxelpose} need images and calibrated cameras at inference and cannot run on keypoint inputs; triangulation of the same 2D keypoints is the reference itself on our rig and is evaluated only on \da{}.

Under zeroing, mean fusion degrades by 19\% because it keeps the dead stream at weight $1/3$. \method{} gives the zeroed view a weight of 0.02 and loses 3.0\% (133.0\,mm), below the oracle that removes the view (152.0\,mm) because the residual remains active; a dropped view costs 3.3\%. \section{Application: Head-Orientation Measurement}
\label{sec:downstream}
Pose accuracy matters for driver monitoring only if it yields valid behavioral signals. We test this for head yaw---the quantity behind mirror checks, glance behavior, and attention estimation---on all 88 in-cabin drives, with identical measurement code for every keypoint source. The aim is to validate the measurement, not to draw behavioral conclusions.

\subsection{Measurement Protocol}
Head yaw is the horizontal angle of the vector from the ear midpoint to the nose in the body frame of Sec.~\ref{sec:canon}, i.e., head rotation relative to the torso; it is smoothed over 0.3\,s and referenced to its median over the drive. A head turn is a $|\mathrm{yaw}|>15^\circ$ excursion lasting at least 0.2\,s. Two references are used. The \emph{independent signal} is the estimator's head rotation matrix, a separate output head that does not depend on nose or ear visibility, brought into the cabin frame with the self-calibrated extrinsics and averaged over the views. The \emph{human reference} is the left/right glance annotation of three raters, an event being kept when at least two agree (1{,}692 events; frame-wise Fleiss $\kappa=0.72$). The annotation tool counts frames at 24\,fps against 29.97\,fps video, so annotated frame indices are rescaled by 1.249 before any comparison; without this step the two time bases drift apart by 52--104\,s (median 69\,s) by the end of a drive. The keypoint streams cover on average the first 77\% of each annotated drive, and all statistics use the covered part.

Single cameras are unreliable sources of head orientation (Table~\ref{tab:downstream}): the front and right cameras fail on six and four drives ($r$ down to 0.20), the signature of steering-wheel and self-occlusion of the ears. Fusion removes these failures, and \method{} raises the worst-case correlation to 0.59. For turns beyond $20^\circ$, \method{} recovers 0.97 of the independent signal, whereas averaging (0.78) and every single view (0.71--0.84) under-estimate turns by 16--29\%: per-view estimates are biased toward a frontal head under self-occlusion, averaging preserves the bias, and the residual removes it (Fig.~\ref{fig:yaw}). More turns therefore cross the threshold (2.65 vs.\ 2.10 per minute), which matters for any count-based attention measure.

\subsection{Agreement with Human Annotations and Stability Across Illumination}
The sign of keypoint yaw during annotated left/right glances agrees with the labelled direction for 96\% of the events with fused keypoints and 95\% with single views, and 93\% of the head turns detected from \method{} overlap an annotated glance. About half of the annotated glances come with a head turn of at least $15^\circ$; the rest are eye-dominant, so glance annotations and head turns are related but distinct measures. The measurement is stable across illumination: with \method{}, the head-turn rate is 2.79, 2.73, 2.48, and 2.61 per minute in the day-high, day-low, night-high, and night-low conditions (Friedman test over the 22 subjects, $p=0.82$), and neither the peak angle ($p=0.89$) nor the yaw spread ($p=0.26$) differs between conditions.

\textbf{Scope.} We validate yaw only: head pitch correlates weakly with the reference pitch for every source (0.32--0.45; 0.40 for \method{}). The independent signal, being a separate output of the same estimator, is only partially independent. And the last quarter of each drive is not yet covered by keypoints.

\section{Lessons for Learning Against Pseudo References}
\label{sec:lessons}

\subsection{Consensus Self-Supervision Collapses View Gating}
\label{sec:collapse}
Our earlier design (v1) was trained against the per-frame coordinate-wise median of its own canonicalized inputs, with view-consistency, bone-length, temporal-smoothness, and contrastive view-alignment terms. Consider any fusion $\hat{p} = \sum_v \alpha_v \bar{P}_v$ with $\alpha$ in the simplex, trained to minimize $\mathbb{E}\,\rho(\hat{p} - T(\bar{P}_{1:V}))$ where the teacher $T$ is a symmetric central-tendency statistic (mean or median) of the same inputs. Under exchangeable per-view noise the objective is symmetric in the views, so its minimizer is $\alpha_v = 1/V$; no input pattern breaks the symmetry, because the teacher moves with the inputs. The prediction is architecture-independent: gates converge to $1/V$ and the output reduces to mean fusion. Every v1 configuration we trained this way---with or without cross-view attention, under different regularization weights, and with a leave-one-view-out teacher---converged to gates of $0.333/0.333/0.333$, scoring 185.7\,mm against 185.8\,mm for mean fusion on its evaluation fold, and the collapse reproduced on \da{}. Adaptive weighting needs information \emph{external} to the views being weighted: here, the triangulation reference and corruption augmentation.

\subsection{Reference-Scale Errors Manufacture Illusory Gains}
\label{sec:scaleartifact}
Against the nominal-extrinsics reference, a 1.9K-parameter fusion model with a temporal head appeared to beat mean fusion by 12.5\%, consistently across folds. The reference is $2.4\times$ too large on average, and the temporal convolution had learned an approximately global inflation of its output (output scale $2.1\times$ the input), which reduces error against a mis-scaled reference while corrupting the pose. The signatures: the gain vanished without the temporal head; scale-sensitive and scale-invariant metrics moved in opposite directions; and zero-shot transfer to \da{}, whose reference has correct scale, \emph{degraded} PA-MPJPE from 23.1 to 28--37\,mm with a 260\,mm rigid-alignment error. After scale anchoring, the same model equals mean fusion (Table~\ref{tab:main}). A scale-consistent reference, or at least a scale-invariant metric, is a prerequisite for claiming learned improvements.

\subsection{Unequal Training Budgets Manufacture Architecture Differences}
Before equalizing budgets, the residual-only model (trained on 6{,}000 steps of 32 $\times$ 64 frames) appeared to beat the full model (3{,}000 steps of 16 $\times$ 32 frames, eight times fewer frames) by 14.3\% against 11.8\% over mean fusion. With one budget they are indistinguishable (128.9 vs.\ 129.1\,mm). Comparisons of fusion architectures are meaningful only at a common, reported budget.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
On a healthy three-camera rig, canonicalized averaging is nearly optimal among methods that only weight views, and a 13\% gain is available to \emph{any} model that adds a reference-supervised residual, including generic regressors fifty times larger. The case for a small gated model is therefore not peak accuracy but what comes with it: tolerance to a noisy camera, inspectable view weights, calibrated per-joint confidence, insensitivity to the adaptation budget on a new rig, and a cost of 28K parameters. Together with a reference that can be rebuilt on any rig without a target, this makes the pipeline deployable: calibrate from the driver's own keypoints, retrain without labels, and run on a CPU.

\textbf{Limitations.} On Procrustes-aligned error the model trails whole-skeleton regressors by 2.6\,mm; combining view gating with a whole-skeleton residual is a natural next design, with the caveat that raw-coordinate residuals transferred worst. No learned residual model transfers zero-shot, so a new vehicle requires the label-free adaptation. The in-cabin reference is triangulated from the same 2D detections that feed the fused streams; \da{}'s independent reference partially fills this gap, but a physically independent sensor would strengthen absolute claims. Hands dominate the error budget (202\,mm vs.\ 48\,mm for non-hand joints) and are at times invisible to all cameras. The gate only partially detects Gaussian corruption; head pitch is not validated; the scale anchor inherits any global bias of the estimator's body prior; and the data come from a driving simulator, so validation on moving vehicles with vibration remains future work.
