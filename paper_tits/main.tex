\begin{abstract}
Driver monitoring systems need stable 3D body pose under occlusion, constrained camera placement, and rapidly changing illumination. Off-the-shelf monocular 3D pose estimators can be deployed per camera, but their outputs live in inconsistent coordinate frames and exhibit view-dependent errors, and obtaining metric 3D ground truth inside a vehicle cabin is impractical. This paper presents a complete, self-calibrated pipeline for fusing synchronized tri-view monocular 3D keypoints. First, we show that a multi-view triangulation reference built with nominal camera extrinsics suffers per-session scale errors of up to $6\times$, and we introduce a per-sequence self-calibration procedure---robust bundle adjustment of the camera extrinsics from the 2D keypoint observations themselves, with the metric scale gauge anchored to the monocular estimator's body-size prior---that reduces the reference reprojection error from 10.3 to 3.6\,px and its cross-session shoulder-width spread from $0.46$--$2.10$\,m to $0.368\pm0.015$\,m without any calibration target or manual 3D annotation. Second, we present \method{}, a lightweight fusion network (28K parameters, $<$0.6\,ms per frame on one CPU thread) that combines masked set attention over any subset of views, corruption-aware gating trained with view-corruption augmentation, a residual refinement head supervised by the self-calibrated triangulation reference, and a per-joint uncertainty output. On 88 subject-environment sequences under four illumination conditions, with subject-disjoint 5-fold evaluation and one training budget shared by every learned method, \method{} reduces MPJPE from 148.6 to 129.1\,mm ($-13.1\%$) and hand error by 13.7\% relative to the strongest training-free fusion, with gains in all four illumination conditions. Against eight further keypoint-level baselines---robust statistics, Procrustes re-alignment, temporal filtering, and MLP, temporal-convolution and transformer regressors with up to $52\times$ more parameters---it matches the best MPJPE (129.7\,mm for a 1.3M-parameter temporal CNN), while the two whole-skeleton regressors reach a lower Procrustes-aligned error (37.8 vs.\ 40.5\,mm). What distinguishes explicit view weighting is robustness: with one camera zeroed, the error of \method{} rises by 3\% while the failed view receives a weight of 0.02, and with 100\,mm noise on one camera it also rises by 3\%, where regressors without view weights degrade by 7--15\%. The predicted uncertainty correlates with the actual error (Spearman $\rho=0.70$). As a downstream check, head yaw measured from the fused keypoints agrees with an independent head-rotation signal on every one of the 88 drives (worst case $r=0.59$), reproduces head-turn amplitude (ratio 0.97, where plain averaging under-estimates it by 22\%), and matches the direction of 96\% of human-annotated left/right glances, whereas a single camera fails on up to six drives. On the public \da{} benchmark, under one leave-one-subject-out protocol for all methods, no learned residual model transfers zero-shot (33.9\,mm PA-MPJPE vs.\ 24.3\,mm for training-free canonicalized fusion); retraining on the new rig---supervised only by triangulation of the rig's own observations, with no manual labels---gives 20.8\,mm (19.4\,mm when the dataset's own reference supervises), the best or tied-best result among all methods, and the error under a noisy camera stays at 26\,mm where the large regressors reach 53--57\,mm. We further contribute two cautionary analyses of broad relevance: a proof that consensus-teacher self-supervision collapses multi-view gating to uniform averaging, and a demonstration of how reference-scale errors manufacture illusory learning gains.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{D}{river} monitoring and in-cabin behavior understanding rely on stable 3D representations of the driver's body. Fine-grained pose supports attention estimation, hand-state analysis, gesture understanding, safety intervention, and higher-level behavior recognition \cite{abouelnaga2017real,zhang2018driver,gorelik2026multiview}. Compared with pose estimation in open scenes, the in-cabin setting is heavily constrained: the driver is seated, cameras are mounted at fixed but vehicle-specific positions, and safety-critical motion is concentrated in a small upper-body workspace. The same constraints create persistent visual challenges---the steering wheel, dashboard, seat, and the driver's own arms repeatedly occlude hands and torso landmarks; viewpoints are limited by the vehicle layout; and illumination changes sharply between day, night, and low-light scenarios.

Monocular 3D pose estimators \cite{toshev2014deeppose,martinez2017simple,mehta2017vnect,kanazawa2018end} are attractive here because they run independently per camera and need no reconstruction infrastructure. Their predictions, however, are depth-ambiguous and view-dependent: each camera returns keypoints in its own root-relative frame, with its own bias pattern, missing joints, and temporal jitter. In driver monitoring these limitations are most pronounced exactly where monitoring needs accuracy most---hands, wrists, and head landmarks affected by steering-wheel occlusion and motion blur. A multi-camera rig observes complementary evidence \cite{iskakov2019learnable,qiu2019cross,tu2020voxelpose}: a joint invisible from the front may be visible from a side camera, and depth ambiguity in one view is often resolved by another. Exploiting this evidence at the \emph{keypoint level}---the natural interface when the per-view estimator is an off-the-shelf component---raises three coupled problems that this paper addresses end to end.

\emph{(1) The reference problem.} Learning and evaluating multi-view fusion requires a 3D reference, yet motion capture is impractical in a cabin, and even checkerboard calibration is difficult to repeat every time a rig is serviced or re-mounted. The common workaround, triangulating 2D keypoints with nominal (assumed) extrinsics, is dangerous in a way that is easy to miss: we show that on our 22-subject dataset the nominal-extrinsics reference has a median body-scale error of $2.4\times$, varying by recording session from $1.2\times$ to $6\times$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:reference}), because the rig geometry differed across sessions. All ``metric'' errors computed against such a reference are dominated by reference-scale error, not pose error---and, as we demonstrate, a learned model will happily absorb the reference's error and appear to improve.

\emph{(2) The fusion problem.} Given well-aligned views, how much can a learned fusion module improve over simple averaging, and where? We answer this under a strictly controlled protocol (identical inputs, references, validity masks, metric code, and training budget for every method) and find a nuanced picture: learned \emph{view weighting} alone offers no accuracy gain---and we prove why its self-supervised variant cannot (Sec.~\ref{sec:collapse})---whereas a \emph{residual refinement} supervised by the self-calibrated reference gives consistent gains, concentrated on the hardest joints (hands). The same supervision also lifts generic regressors: a temporal CNN and a lifting MLP with roughly fifty times more parameters reach the same MPJPE and a lower Procrustes-aligned error. Accuracy on clean inputs therefore does not single out an architecture; cost and behavior under corrupted views do.

\emph{(3) The robustness and transfer problem.} A deployed system must tolerate a failed or corrupted camera, and a method developed on one rig must be transferable to another vehicle. We show that corruption-aware training lets every learned model survive a dead camera, but that only models with explicit per-view weights also survive a \emph{noisy} one; and that residual refinement is inherently rig-specific---every learned residual model degrades under zero-shot transfer, and accuracy is restored, without any manual labels, by re-running the adaptation on the target rig in minutes.

This paper is a substantially revised and corrected successor of an earlier conference submission describing TriPoseFusion. In re-auditing that system we found that its headline improvements were artifacts of evaluation defects (an inverted Procrustes rotation and a degenerate canonicalization fallback that collapsed frames to the origin) and of the reference-scale error above. We document these findings as analyses (Secs.~\ref{sec:collapse} and \ref{sec:scaleartifact}) because they concern failure modes that can affect any work that trains or evaluates against pseudo ground truth.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A self-calibrated triangulation reference for in-cabin rigs.} Per-sequence robust bundle adjustment of camera extrinsics from the 2D keypoint observations themselves, with the scale gauge anchored to the monocular estimator's body-size prior. No calibration target, no manual 3D annotation. Reference reprojection error drops from 10.3 to 3.6\,px, front-view utilization rises from 8\% to 45\%, and the cross-session shoulder-width spread collapses from std 0.37\,m to 0.015\,m (Tables~\ref{tab:gtquality} and \ref{tab:gtbreak}).
\item \textbf{\method{}, a lightweight, deployment-oriented fusion network.} Masked set attention over the available views (any subset of 1--3 cameras), corruption-aware gating trained with view-corruption augmentation, residual refinement supervised by the self-calibrated reference, and a per-joint Laplace uncertainty output. 27.9K parameters; $<$0.6\,ms per frame on a single CPU thread.
\item \textbf{A controlled benchmark and an honest accounting of where learning helps.} Under subject-disjoint 5-fold evaluation with one training budget for all learned methods and eight additional keypoint-level baselines, \method{} improves MPJPE by 13.1\% (148.6 to 129.1\,mm), hand error by 13.7\%, and PCK@100\,mm by $+4.8$ points over the strongest training-free fusion, with gains in all four illumination conditions. View weighting alone contributes nothing; high-capacity whole-skeleton regressors match the MPJPE and have a 2.6\,mm lower Procrustes-aligned error at about 50$\times$ the parameter count; under a zeroed camera every augmented model stays within 6\% of its clean error (vs.\ 19\% degradation for averaging), but under a noisy camera only the gated models do (3\% vs.\ 7--15\%). A downstream head-orientation check shows the fused keypoints yield a valid head-yaw signal with correct amplitude on all 88 drives, where single cameras fail on up to six.
\item \textbf{Cross-rig validation on \da{}} \cite{martin2019drive} under a single leave-one-subject-out protocol for zero-shot and in-domain evaluation: training-free canonicalized fusion transfers unchanged (24.3\,mm PA-MPJPE); no learned residual model does (33.9--73\,mm); and the fully label-free adaptation recipe---supervised only by DLT triangulation of the rig's own 2D keypoints---brings \method{} to 20.8\,mm (19.4\,mm with the dataset's own reference), the best or tied-best among all compared methods and the least sensitive to the adaptation budget.
\item \textbf{Two cautionary analyses}: a proof that consensus-teacher self-supervision makes uniform view weighting the loss-optimal solution (explaining ``gate collapse'' observations), and a quantified case study of how reference-scale errors manufacture illusory learning gains that do not transfer.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Monocular and Video-Based 3D Pose Estimation}
Monocular 3D pose estimation recovers joints by direct image-to-pose regression, 2D-to-3D lifting, or parametric body-model fitting \cite{toshev2014deeppose,martinez2017simple,zhou2017towards,mehta2017vnect,kanazawa2018end}. Video-based models exploit temporal context to reduce per-frame ambiguity and jitter \cite{pavllo20193d,kocabas2020vibe}. These approaches are directly useful for driver monitoring because they can be deployed on each cabin camera independently. Single-view recovery nevertheless remains ill-posed under steering-wheel occlusion, truncation by the image border, viewpoint bias, and illumination extremes; the resulting 3D keypoints contain missing joints, inconsistent scale, incorrect depth ordering, and high-frequency jitter. We treat such outputs as noisy observations and study how to combine them across views, rather than proposing another monocular estimator.

\subsection{Multi-View Pose Estimation and Keypoint-Level Fusion}
Classical and learned multi-view methods triangulate 2D detections with calibrated cameras \cite{hartley1997triangulation,iskakov2019learnable}, exchange image features or heatmaps across views \cite{qiu2019cross,he2020epipolar}, or aggregate evidence volumetrically \cite{tu2020voxelpose,bultmann2021real,nogueira2024markerless}. These methods assume image access and calibrated cameras at inference time, and volumetric aggregation carries a substantial computational cost that is difficult to justify on automotive edge hardware. Our interface is deliberately narrower: the fusion stage receives only per-view 3D keypoints from an existing estimator, requires no calibration at deployment (calibration is used only to construct the training reference), and runs in microseconds per frame. This keypoint-level setting exposes difficulties that image-level methods do not face---per-view root-relative frames that are mutually inconsistent, missing joints, and time-varying, joint-dependent view reliability---and, as we show, it changes which components actually matter: with well-normalized inputs, sophisticated cross-view weighting is worth little, while residual correction and robustness to view failure are worth a lot.

\subsection{Self-Supervision, Pseudo Labels, and Their Failure Modes}
When metric 3D labels are unavailable, prior work substitutes reprojection consistency, multi-view agreement, bone-length priors, temporal smoothness, or triangulated pseudo labels \cite{rhodin2018learning,kocabas2019self,iskakov2019learnable,wandt2021canonpose,fan2023humanm3}. Our reference construction belongs to this family but differs in two respects: extrinsics are refined \emph{per recording session} directly from the human-keypoint correspondences (no calibration target), and the metric scale gauge---unobservable from uncalibrated multi-view geometry---is anchored to the monocular estimator's body prior and validated against anatomy. We further contribute two failure-mode analyses relevant to this entire line of work: consensus-teacher objectives provably drive adaptive view weighting to uniform averaging (Sec.~\ref{sec:collapse}), and scale errors in a pseudo reference are silently absorbed by refinement networks, inflating apparent gains that evaporate under cross-rig transfer (Sec.~\ref{sec:scaleartifact}).

\subsection{Driver Monitoring Datasets and In-Cabin Pose}
In-cabin behavior datasets support distraction and activity recognition \cite{abouelnaga2017real,zhang2018driver,gorelik2026multiview}; \da{} \cite{martin2019drive} additionally provides multi-view recordings with OpenPose-triangulated 3D references and serves as our cross-rig testbed. Our own dataset complements it with a three-camera RGB rig, 22 subjects, four illumination conditions, and a 52-keypoint protocol that includes both hands---the body parts that dominate the in-cabin error budget yet are frequently truncated or occluded.

\section{Self-Calibrated Triangulation Reference}
\label{sec:reference}

\subsection{Rig, Estimator, and Triangulation Pipeline}
Our rig has three cameras (front, left, right) observing the seated driver (Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset}). Each view runs the same off-the-shelf monocular estimator (SAM3D-Body), which outputs 70 2D and 3D keypoints per frame; a 52-keypoint subset (5 head, 5 shoulder/neck, and $2\times21$ hand keypoints) is used throughout. The reference is built by triangulating the synchronized per-view 2D keypoints \cite{hartley1997triangulation}. For each joint and frame: every subset of $\geq 2$ views with valid, in-bounds observations is triangulated by DLT; the subset with the lowest mean reprojection error is retained (ties broken toward more views); candidates with non-positive depth in any used camera are rejected; and points whose best mean reprojection error exceeds 40\,px are marked invalid. Per-point quality statistics---multi-view and per-view reprojection errors, leave-one-view-out 3D displacement, and the DLT condition number---are stored alongside the reference, so that downstream users can filter or weight by reference confidence.

Intrinsics were estimated once per camera. Extrinsics are the crux, and the remainder of this section is about getting them---and the metric scale---right without a calibration target.

\subsection{The Nominal-Extrinsics Problem}
\label{sec:nominal}
The extrinsics were originally \emph{constructed} from a nominal layout: look-at poses derived from measured target distances and a 0.70\,m left--right baseline. Reprojection statistics of the resulting reference look tolerable (mean 10.3\,px; Table~\ref{tab:gtbreak}), which is precisely why the defect below went unnoticed.

Auditing the reference against anatomy exposed it. The median triangulated shoulder width across the 88 sequences was 0.84\,m---more than twice a plausible human value---and ranged from 0.46\,m to 2.10\,m depending on the \emph{subject}, i.e., on the recording session (Table~\ref{tab:gtquality}, first row). The estimator's own metric body prior was, by contrast, extremely stable: median shoulder width 0.371\,m with a spread below 0.06\,m across all views, subjects, and illumination conditions. The scale error was independent of illumination but strongly session-dependent, implying that the rig was re-positioned between sessions while a single nominal geometry was assumed. Because the induced error is mostly scale-like, it poisons every metric error computed against the reference while remaining nearly invisible to reprojection-based quality checks: with a wrong baseline, reprojections stay consistent while the reconstruction is uniformly mis-scaled.

\subsection{Per-Sequence Robust Extrinsic Refinement}
\label{sec:persequence}
We therefore refine extrinsics \emph{per sequence}, using the 2D keypoint observations themselves as correspondences:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fix the left camera as the gauge; parameterize the front and right cameras by 6-DoF perturbations of the nominal poses.
\item Sample $\sim$40 frames evenly from the sequence; collect all joints observed in $\geq 2$ views.
\item Minimize the Huber-robustified reprojection error of DLT-triangulated points over the 12 camera parameters (variable-projection form: structure is re-triangulated inside the residual), with a soft prior toward the nominal layout.
\item Reject observations with residuals above 30\,px against the round-1 solution and re-optimize.
\end{enumerate}
Across 88 sequences the refinement rotates the nominal camera orientations by a median of 21$^\circ$ (front) and 31$^\circ$ (right)---up to 53$^\circ$---confirming that the nominal layout was substantially wrong and different in every session. On held-out frames, the mean reprojection error drops from 9.6 to 3.4\,px and the front camera's participation in accepted triangulations rises from 8\% to 45\%; the whole procedure takes well under a minute per sequence on a CPU.

\subsection{Anchoring the Metric Scale Gauge}
\label{sec:anchor}
Rotation refinement alone cannot fix the scale. With unknown extrinsics, the global scale of the reconstruction is a gauge freedom---scaling the scene and the camera centers about the fixed camera leaves every reprojection unchanged---and pinning it with the nominal 0.70\,m baseline is exactly what failed: back-solving from the refined geometry, the true baselines varied from 0.25 to 1.07\,m (median 0.80\,m) across sessions. We instead anchor the gauge \emph{anatomically}: each sequence's reconstruction is rescaled about the fixed camera so that its median triangulated shoulder width equals the monocular estimator's median metric shoulder width for that same sequence. This choice (i) costs nothing in reprojection error, by gauge invariance; (ii) makes the reference's metric scale consistent with the very streams being fused, so that fusion errors measure pose, not scale disagreement; and (iii) is validated by convergence: the resulting shoulder widths concentrate at $0.368\pm0.015$\,m across all 88 sequences, in agreement with both the estimator prior (0.371\,m) and human anatomy. The anchor inherits any global bias of the estimator's body prior; scale-invariant metrics (PA-MPJPE) are unaffected, and a single tape measurement of one true baseline would convert the anchor to an external metric scale if required.

\subsection{Problem Setting}
At inference, \method{} receives synchronized monocular 3D keypoint streams $P_v \in \mathbb{R}^{T\times J\times 3}$ from the available views $v \in \mathcal{V}$, $1 \le |\mathcal{V}| \le 3$, with $J{=}52$, and outputs a fused sequence $P^{\mathrm{out}}$ plus a per-joint uncertainty $\sigma$. No camera calibration and no images are needed at inference; the calibrated reference of Sec.~\ref{sec:reference} is used only as the training signal.

\subsection{Robust Body-Centered Canonicalization}
Each view is first mapped into a body-centered frame. With neck position $o_t$ and shoulder positions $s^l_t, s^r_t$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_t = \frac{s^r_t - s^l_t}{\|s^r_t - s^l_t\|},\quad
\mathbf{d}_t = \tfrac{1}{2}(s^l_t+s^r_t) - o_t,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_t = \frac{\mathbf{x}_t \times \mathbf{d}_t}{\|\mathbf{x}_t \times \mathbf{d}_t\|},\quad
\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{z}_t \times \mathbf{x}_t,\quad
\bar{P}^{t,j}_v = R_t^\top (P^{t,j}_v - o_t),
\end{equation}
with $R_t = [\mathbf{x}_t, \mathbf{y}_t, \mathbf{z}_t]$. Two robustness rules matter in practice. If a frame's shoulder estimate is degenerate---coincident points or an anatomically implausible width ($<\epsilon$ or $>1.0$\,m)---the shoulder axis of the \emph{temporally nearest valid frame} is reused; substituting a zero vector (as an earlier implementation did) collapses $R_t$ to rank zero and maps the entire frame to the origin. If $\mathbf{x}_t$ and $\mathbf{d}_t$ become near-parallel, a fixed reference axis restores full rank. Canonicalization is what makes per-view streams commensurable: it reduces the cross-view disagreement of raw monocular outputs from the 2\,m scale (root-relative camera frames) to the 0.15\,m scale on our data.

\subsection{Masked Set Attention over Views}
For each joint $j$ and frame $t$, the $|\mathcal{V}|$ canonicalized observations form a token set. Each token is
\begin{equation}
u^{t,j}_v = W_{\mathrm{in}}\big[\bar{P}^{t,j}_v;\; \delta^{t,j}_v;\; m^{t,j}_v\big] + e^{\mathrm{view}}_v + e^{\mathrm{joint}}_j,
\end{equation}
where $\delta^{t,j}_v = \|\bar{P}^{t,j}_v - \mu^{t,j}\|$ is the distance to the finite-view mean $\mu^{t,j}$ (a cross-view-consistency cue), $m^{t,j}_v$ a validity flag, and $e^{\mathrm{view}}, e^{\mathrm{joint}}$ learned embeddings. Two transformer encoder layers ($d{=}32$, 4 heads) operate \emph{across the view set} with key masking of invalid views. Attention over a set is permutation-equivariant \cite{zaheer2017deep}, so the same network accepts any subset of cameras; a view that drops out at runtime is excluded exactly like a view that was never installed.

A linear head maps each attended token to a gate logit $g^{t,j}_v$; a masked softmax yields per-joint, per-frame view weights and the gated fusion:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{t,j,v} = \frac{\exp g^{t,j}_v}{\sum_{v'\in\mathcal{V}_{t,j}} \exp g^{t,j}_{v'}},\qquad
\hat{p}_{t,j} = \sum_{v} \alpha_{t,j,v}\, \bar{P}^{t,j}_v .
\end{equation}
The attention-pooled feature $h_{t,j} = \sum_v \alpha_{t,j,v} u^{t,j}_v$ is then processed by one transformer layer across the $J$ joints (skeleton context, e.g., wrist evidence informing finger corrections) and a depthwise temporal convolution (kernel 5), and two zero-initialized heads output a residual correction and a Laplace log-scale:
\begin{equation}
p^{\mathrm{out}}_{t,j} = \hat{p}_{t,j} + \Delta_{t,j},\qquad \sigma_{t,j} = \exp(s_{t,j}).
\end{equation}
The residual head is the component that can move joints \emph{away} from all observations---for example, correcting a shared depth bias of the monocular estimator---which pure convex weighting cannot do.

\subsection{Training: Reference Supervision, Corruption Augmentation, Uncertainty}
\label{sec:training}
The only loss is a masked Laplace negative log-likelihood against the self-calibrated reference $g_{t,j}$ over its valid joints $\Omega$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{(t,j)\in\Omega} \Big( \|p^{\mathrm{out}}_{t,j}-g_{t,j}\|_1 \, e^{-s_{t,j}} + 3\, s_{t,j} \Big),
\label{eq:nll}
\end{equation}
which trains the residual head and calibrates the uncertainty jointly \cite{kendall2017uncertainties}. During training, with probability 0.5 per sample, one randomly chosen view is corrupted: dropped (masked out), zeroed (a constant stream---an undetected sensor failure), or perturbed with $\sigma{=}100$\,mm Gaussian noise. This \emph{corruption augmentation} is what teaches the gate to detect and down-weight unreliable views; without an external supervision signal and corrupted examples, the gate has no incentive to encode reliability (Secs.~\ref{sec:collapse}, \ref{sec:robustness}).

\textbf{Implementation.} Deployed capacity is $d{=}32$ (27.9K parameters). Training uses Adam ($10^{-3}$, no decay), 6{,}000 steps of 32 windows of 64 consecutive frames (about 23 passes over the training frames) sampled from fp16-cached canonicalized sequences, a fixed seed, and evaluation of the final checkpoint only (no model selection on the evaluation fold). One fold trains in about 13 minutes on one GPU including periodic validation; inference needs no GPU. Three deliberate simplicity choices follow from our analyses: no consensus self-supervision (Sec.~\ref{sec:collapse}); no contrastive view-alignment loss, which actively erases the inter-view differences the gate must read; and no extra capacity or longer training, neither of which helps (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Why Consensus Self-Supervision Collapses Gating}
\label{sec:collapse}
The predecessor system (v1) trained its fused output against the per-frame coordinate-wise \emph{median} of its own canonicalized inputs, with auxiliary self-referential terms (view consistency toward the inputs, bone lengths measured against its own pre-refinement output, temporal smoothness) and a contrastive view-alignment loss. Consider any fusion of the form $\hat{p} = \sum_v \alpha_v \bar{P}_v$ with $\alpha$ in the simplex, trained to minimize $\mathbb{E}\,\rho(\hat{p} - T(\bar{P}_{1:V}))$ where the teacher $T$ is a symmetric central-tendency statistic (mean or median) of the same inputs. Under exchangeable per-view noise, the objective is symmetric in the views and its minimizer over $\alpha$ is the uniform weight $\alpha_v = 1/V$; no input pattern breaks the symmetry, because the teacher moves with the inputs. The prediction is architecture-independent and specific: gates converge to exactly $1/V$ and the output reduces to mean fusion. Empirically, every retrained v1 configuration---with or without cross-view attention, with different regularization weights, and with a leave-one-view-out teacher variant---converged to gates of $0.333/0.333/0.333$; under our unified protocol the v1 model scores 185.7\,mm MPJPE versus 185.8\,mm for plain mean fusion on its evaluation fold, and the collapse reproduced zero-shot on \da{}. Adaptive multi-view weighting requires information \emph{external} to the views being weighted: in \method{}, the triangulation reference and the corruption augmentation.

\subsection{How Reference-Scale Errors Manufacture Illusory Gains}
\label{sec:scaleartifact}
Against the nominal-extrinsics reference (Table~\ref{tab:gtquality}, first row), a 1.9K-parameter fusion model with a temporal head appeared to beat mean fusion by 12.5\% MPJPE, consistently across folds. The gain was an artifact. The reference is on average $2.4\times$ too large, so the network's temporal convolution learned an approximately global inflation of its output---the measured output scale was $2.1\times$ the input scale---which reduces error against a mis-scaled reference while corrupting the pose itself. The tell-tale signatures were: (i) the entire gain disappeared when the temporal head was removed; (ii) scale-sensitive and scale-invariant metrics moved in opposite directions; and (iii) zero-shot transfer to \da{}, whose reference has correct scale, \emph{degraded} from 23.1 to 28--37\,mm PA-MPJPE with a rigid-alignment error of 260\,mm. After scale anchoring, the same model is indistinguishable from mean fusion (148.6 vs.\ 148.6\,mm; Table~\ref{tab:main}). Pseudo-ground-truth supervision is widespread in pose estimation; this case study shows that a scale-consistent reference (or at minimum a scale-invariant metric) is a prerequisite for claiming learned improvements.

\subsection{Protocol, Metrics, and Baselines}
\textbf{Protocol.} Subject-disjoint GroupKFold with 5 folds on the in-cabin dataset; leave-one-subject-out (3 folds) on \da{}. Every sequence is scored by a model that never saw its subject, on the whole sequence. \emph{All learned methods share one training budget}---6{,}000 steps of 32 $\times$ 64-frame windows, the same corruption augmentation ($p{=}0.5$), a fixed seed, and the final checkpoint---because we found that unequal budgets alone produce differences as large as those between architectures. All methods, training-free and learned, consume identical cached canonicalized inputs, the identical reference, identical validity masks, and identical metric code; this ``same-path'' discipline matters, since mixing two canonicalization implementations between method and baseline rows can alone fabricate double-digit relative differences on hand joints.

\textbf{Metrics.} All errors are reported in millimetres. MPJPE is the mean Euclidean error over all valid joint instances of a sequence (joints pooled over frames, so frames with more visible hand joints weigh more), averaged over sequences and then over folds. PA-MPJPE uses the correct least-squares similarity solution \cite{umeyama1991least} (an inverted rotation in a previous implementation systematically inflated PA errors---for well-aligned shapes a transposed Procrustes rotation can even exceed the unaligned error, which is how the defect was originally noticed) over exactly the same joint mask. PCK@100\,mm is the fraction of valid joints within 100\,mm. Joint-group breakdowns use head (5 joints), shoulders--neck (5), all non-hand joints (10), and hands (42). Tables give the mean over folds; fold-to-fold standard deviations (24--32\,mm MPJPE) reflect subject difficulty and are common to all methods, so differences between methods are tested by paired per-sequence comparison with a subject-level bootstrap.

\textbf{Baselines.} \emph{Training-free}: per-view single baselines; the per-sequence \emph{oracle} best single view (a diagnostic upper bound for view selection, not a deployable method); canonicalized mean and median fusion; the geometric median over views (Weiszfeld iterations \cite{weiszfeld1937point}); leave-one-out consensus, which discards the view farthest from the other two when it deviates by more than 100\,mm and twice their spread, in the spirit of RANSAC \cite{fischler1981random}; mean fusion followed by temporal Gaussian smoothing ($\sigma{=}2$ frames); and generalized-Procrustes re-alignment of the views before averaging \cite{gower1975generalized}. \emph{Learned}, all trained on the same reference with the same augmentation and parameterized as a zero-initialized residual on mean fusion so that none starts below the averaging baseline: a per-joint MLP on the concatenated views (Concat-MLP, 6.1K parameters); a whole-skeleton lifting MLP in the style of \cite{martinez2017simple} (Lifting-MLP, 1.46M); a dilated temporal CNN in the style of \cite{pavllo20193d} with a 29-frame receptive field (Temporal-CNN, 1.31M); a transformer over the joints in the style of the spatial encoder of \cite{zheng2021poseformer} (Skeleton-Transformer, 105K); the v1 backbone (per-view encoder, cross-view attention, gate, dilated TCN; 261K) retrained with our supervision; a pure convex view-weighting model (1.9K; the keypoint-level counterpart of learned-confidence triangulation \cite{iskakov2019learnable}); and a residual-only model whose 8K-parameter head reads the raw per-view coordinates behind an independent per-view gate. Image-based multi-view estimators \cite{iskakov2019learnable,tu2020voxelpose} require images or heatmaps and calibrated cameras at inference and cannot be run on keypoint-only inputs; triangulation of the same 2D keypoints is the reference itself on our rig and is therefore reported only on \da{}, where an independent reference exists (Sec.~\ref{sec:driveandact}).

Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the central accuracy picture; four observations. \emph{First}, canonicalized mean fusion is a strong baseline: it beats every individual camera and sits within 3\% of the per-sequence oracle camera, and none of the four further training-free schemes improves on it on clean inputs ($-0.3\%$ to $+1.3\%$)---robust statistics and re-alignment pay off only under corruption (Sec.~\ref{sec:robustness}). \emph{Second}, learned view weighting adds nothing: the convex-weighting model ends at 148.7\,mm with near-uniform weights ($0.34/0.34/0.33$), consistent with Sec.~\ref{sec:collapse} and with the small selection headroom, and now confirmed even under reference supervision. \emph{Third}, residual refinement is the accuracy lever, and it is not specific to our architecture: every model able to move a joint away from the per-view observations improves MPJPE by 9--13\%. \method{} (129.1\,mm, $-13.1\%$) and the residual-only model (128.9\,mm) are the best together with the Temporal-CNN (129.7\,mm) and the Lifting-MLP (130.7\,mm); a paired per-sequence comparison with a subject-level bootstrap finds no MPJPE difference between \method{} and these three (95\% intervals of the difference $[-2.8,+2.9]$, $[-2.0,+3.7]$, and $[-1.7,+5.7]$\,mm), whereas \method{} is better than the Skeleton-Transformer ($5.8$\,mm, $[2.9,9.2]$) and the retrained v1 backbone ($6.4$\,mm, $[1.5,12.3]$); its $2.9$\,mm margin over the Concat-MLP is borderline ($[0.0,5.8]$). \emph{Fourth}, on Procrustes-aligned error the two whole-skeleton regressors are better than every other method: 37.8 and 37.9\,mm against 40.5\,mm, a paired difference of $2.6$\,mm ($[0.7,4.6]$; lower on 70 of 88 sequences) for the Temporal-CNN and $2.5$\,mm ($[1.2,4.0]$) for the Lifting-MLP. The regressors' advantage therefore lies in the internal consistency of the predicted shape, which a network that sees the whole flattened skeleton can enforce and a per-joint token model cannot; widening \method{} does not close it (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}). We report this as a limitation rather than tune toward it: these regressors need 47--52$\times$ the parameters and, as shown next, are not robust to a noisy view.

Per joint group (Table~\ref{tab:groups}, top), the improvement of \method{} over mean fusion concentrates where monitoring needs it most---hands ($233.5\to201.6$\,mm, $-13.7\%$) and head ($-9.8\%$)---with shoulders/neck essentially unchanged ($22.0\to22.5$\,mm; these near-origin joints define the canonical frame). Per illumination (Table~\ref{tab:groups}, bottom), the gain is present in all four conditions ($-12\%$ to $-15\%$), largest at night-low; fusion accuracy itself is stable from day to night, supporting the multi-view premise under illumination change.

\emph{A noisy camera.} Here the architectures separate. \subsection{Cross-Rig Evaluation on \da{}}
\label{sec:driveandact}
\da{} provides an independent rig, an independent reference (multi-view OpenPose triangulation, i.e., a source unrelated to our estimator), and unseen subjects.

\textbf{Zero-shot, training-free.} Running the same monocular estimator per view and our canonicalize-and-average fusion on the 1\,fps triplets yields, pooled over 6{,}673 frames: body14 MPJPE 55.8\,mm / PA-MPJPE 31.3\,mm, upper-body-12 53.6/23.8\,mm; fusion beats the best single view by 8\% (body14 PA) to 13\% (upper12 PA), and PCK@50\,mm is 42\% (94\% @100\,mm). For calibration of expectations: fully supervised methods trained on \da{} report 22.6--30.4\,mm MPJPE; our numbers come from a system that has never seen this dataset, requires no calibration, and fuses in microseconds.

\textbf{Calibrated triangulation baseline.} \da{} also lets us report what calibrated geometry achieves on the same inputs---and, uniquely, \emph{absolute} errors in the dataset's metric frame. Direct DLT triangulation of the per-view SAM3D 2D keypoints (undistorted, with the same 40\,px acceptance filter as Sec.~\ref{sec:reference}) attains 32.5\,mm body14 MPJPE (upper12: 22.2\,mm; PA 29.0/18.0\,mm) with no alignment of any kind---at the edge of the fully supervised range; the median residual between projected reference and detected 2D is 15.5\,px, confirming calibration and synchronization. Removing any one view raises the error by 10--40\% (worst pair, front$+$right: 45.5\,mm), quantifying the third camera's contribution. Triangulation, however, requires calibrated extrinsics at inference time, which our calibration-free fusion does not; in the canonical frame the two are close in PA terms (29.0 vs.\ 31.3\,mm body14).

\textbf{One protocol for all learned methods.} All model comparisons on \da{} use leave-one-subject-out evaluation over the three subjects (two 60\,s runs each), whole-sequence scoring against the official reference on the nine joints shared with our keypoint set (head, shoulders, wrists). \emph{Zero-shot} numbers evaluate the five in-cabin fold models of each method on every \da{} validation fold and average them. \emph{In-domain} numbers retrain every method on the two training subjects with the in-cabin recipe at 1{,}500 steps, a budget fixed for this dataset before any baseline was run (Table~\ref{tab:da}).

\textbf{Zero-shot: learned residuals do not transfer.} Training-free mean fusion scores 24.7/24.3\,mm (MPJPE/PA) under this protocol, and the convex-weighting model transfers unchanged (25.0/24.3\,mm). No model with a residual does: \method{} degrades to 49.6/33.9\,mm, the Temporal-CNN to 50.3/35.6\,mm, the Lifting-MLP to 59.0/42.9\,mm, and the two models whose residual reads raw per-view coordinates collapse (residual-only 73.9/70.1\,mm, with a range of 34--99\,mm PA over the five source models; Concat-MLP 78.0/73.3\,mm). The corrections these models learned encode rig-and-estimator-specific systematic error, and the learned view priors even change across rigs (front is the least-trusted view on our rig, $\alpha_f\!\approx\!0.19$, but as trusted as the best view after adaptation on \da{}, $\alpha_f\!\approx\!0.38$): view reliability is a property of the installation, not of the method. \textbf{Label-free in-domain adaptation.} The correct deployment procedure is therefore to re-run the adaptation on the target rig. In the fully label-free version, supervision is the DLT triangulation of the rig's own 2D keypoints (undistorted, with the 40\,px acceptance filter of Sec.~\ref{sec:reference}); since \da{} ships calibrated cameras, the extrinsic refinement of Sec.~\ref{sec:persequence} is not needed here, and the official 3D reference is used only for evaluation. \method{} reaches 22.3/20.8\,mm ($-10\%$/$-14\%$ vs.\ training-free fusion); supervising with the dataset's own reference instead---an upper bound on reference quality---yields 21.2/19.4\,mm ($-14\%$/$-20\%$). The gap of 1.1/1.4\,mm is consistent with the self-built reference's own agreement with the official one (14--22\,mm PA per run), i.e., the adaptation gain grows with reference quality.

Against the baselines retrained identically, \method{} and the residual-only model are best or tied-best in both settings: 21.1--21.2/19.1--19.4\,mm with the dataset reference, against 22.5--22.6/19.7--20.1\,mm for the two large regressors, and 22.2--22.4/20.4--20.8\,mm for all four with the rig reference. With three subjects, differences of this size are within the fold-to-fold variation ($\pm$2--4\,mm), so we claim parity rather than superiority; the PA disadvantage observed in-cabin does not appear on this hand-free joint set, although on the wrists alone the whole-skeleton regressors remain lower (26.4--27.6 vs.\ 29.5--30.9\,mm). Two further properties favor the light gated models. \emph{(i) Budget sensitivity.} With 6{,}000 instead of 1{,}500 steps on these 7{,}200 training frames, \method{} is unchanged (21.5/19.9\,mm) while the Temporal-CNN and the Lifting-MLP overfit (24.6/22.3 and 24.0/21.9\,mm). \emph{(ii) Corrupted cameras.} Under a zeroed camera all adapted models hold (24--28\,mm, against 84\,mm for averaging), but under a noisy camera \method{} stays at 26.0\,mm against 52.8--56.7\,mm for the large regressors, which is close to averaging itself (59.2\,mm)---the in-cabin finding of Sec.~\ref{sec:robustness} reproduced on an independent rig.

\subsection{Uncertainty Calibration}
The Laplace scale output orders errors well: the Spearman correlation between predicted scale and realized per-joint error is $0.70$ on the in-cabin folds (0.69--0.73 across the architecture variants of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) and $0.45$--$0.51$ on adapted \da{} (label-free / dataset-reference supervision). Thresholding by confidence is directly useful downstream: the most-confident decile of joints carries 13\,mm mean error versus 234\,mm for the least-confident decile (146\,mm overall); on adapted \da{}, 11\,mm versus 35\,mm. A monitoring stack can thus suppress unreliable joints per frame---a capability the regression baselines and the training-free schemes do not offer.

\subsection{Downstream Utility: Head-Orientation Measurement}
\label{sec:downstream}
To verify that pose-level gains translate into a DMS-relevant signal, we evaluate head orientation---the quantity behind mirror checks, glance behavior, and attention estimation---on all 88 in-cabin drives, with identical measurement code for every keypoint source. Head yaw is computed from the five head keypoints (nose, eyes, ears) in the canonical body frame; a head turn is a $|\mathrm{yaw}|>15^\circ$ excursion lasting $\geq0.2$\,s. Two references are used. The \emph{independent signal} is the per-view estimator's head rotation matrix (a different output head than the keypoints), brought into the cabin frame through the self-calibrated extrinsics and averaged over the three views; it does not depend on nose/ear visibility. The \emph{human reference} is the left/right glance annotation of three raters (1{,}692 events). The annotation tool counts frames at 24\,fps whereas the videos run at 29.97\,fps, so annotated frame indices are rescaled by 1.249 before any comparison; without this step the comparison is misaligned by up to a minute per drive and direction agreement falls to chance. The keypoint streams cover on average the first 77\% of each annotated drive, and all statistics are computed on the covered part.

Fusion removes these failures---mean fusion and \method{} have none---and \method{} raises the worst-case correlation to 0.59. Its advantage is amplitude. When the independent signal indicates a turn beyond $20^\circ$, yaw from \method{} has 0.97 of its magnitude, whereas averaging (0.78) and every single view (0.71--0.84) under-estimate turns by 16--29\%: per-view estimates are biased toward a frontal head pose under self-occlusion, averaging preserves that bias, and the residual head removes it (Fig.~\ref{fig:yaw}). As a consequence more turns cross the $15^\circ$ threshold (2.65 vs.\ 2.10 per minute)---a difference that matters for any count-based attention measure.

Against the human reference, the sign of keypoint yaw during annotated left/right glances agrees with the labelled direction in 96\% of the events for all fused sources and 95\% for single views; 93\% of the head turns detected from \method{} overlap an annotated glance, and about half of the annotated glances are accompanied by a head rotation of at least $15^\circ$, the remainder being eye-dominant. The measurement is stable across illumination: head-turn rate, peak angle, and yaw spread do not differ between the four lighting conditions (Friedman test, $p\geq0.26$), consistent with the illumination breakdown of Table~\ref{tab:groups}. Three caveats apply. The validation targets the head signal; utility for hand-related functions is supported only indirectly, by the per-joint hand improvements. The independent signal, although a separate output head and multi-view consistent, comes from the same estimator family and is therefore only partially independent. And head \emph{pitch} is not validated: its correlation with the reference pitch is low for every source (0.32--0.45) and lower for \method{} (0.40) than for mean fusion (0.45), so we restrict our claims to yaw.

\textbf{Components.} Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates the contributions. Removing corruption augmentation leaves clean accuracy unchanged but destroys robustness (Sec.~\ref{sec:robustness}). Removing the skeleton-attention layer leaves MPJPE unchanged (128.7\,mm) but raises PA-MPJPE from 40.5 to 42.7\,mm (paired difference $2.2$\,mm, $[0.4,4.4]$): attention across joints does not move joints closer to the reference on average, it makes the predicted shape more coherent---the same quantity on which whole-skeleton regressors lead. Removing the temporal convolution costs 2.1\,mm MPJPE (131.2\,mm) at unchanged PA-MPJPE. Letting the residual head read the raw per-view coordinates (the hybrid and the residual-only rows) gives the same in-cabin accuracy but is what makes zero-shot transfer collapse (Sec.~\ref{sec:driveandact}).

\textbf{Capacity.} More parameters do not help the token-based design: widening \method{} to $d{=}64$ (105K) or $d{=}128$ (407K) changes MPJPE by $+3$\,mm and leaves PA-MPJPE where it was, so the PA advantage of the whole-skeleton regressors in Table~\ref{tab:main} is structural rather than a matter of size. The v1 backbone at its original capacity (870K) reaches 136.4/40.9\,mm, no better than its 261K version (135.8/41.3\,mm).

\textbf{Training budget and window.} Table~\ref{tab:budget} shows that the common budget is not a handicap for our models. Training four times longer does not help: the MPJPE of \method{} rises from 129.1 to 133.6\,mm with unchanged PA-MPJPE, and the residual-only model is flat. Varying the training-window length from 16 to 256 frames at a fixed number of frames per step moves MPJPE by at most 1.4\% for all three models; the Temporal-CNN is no worse with 16-frame windows, which are shorter than its receptive field, indicating that it acts as a per-frame whole-skeleton regressor on these data. Adjacent checkpoints of one fold differ by up to 3\%, so differences below about 1\,mm in any of our tables should not be interpreted.

\textbf{Efficiency.} \method{} runs in $<$0.6\,ms per frame on one CPU thread (residual-only: 0.08\,ms; both fit in $<$1\,MB) and needs no GPU at inference. Training one in-cabin fold takes about 13 minutes on one GPU, and adapting to a new rig as in Sec.~\ref{sec:driveandact} under ten minutes.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Our results reframe what a learned in-cabin fusion module should claim. On a healthy three-camera rig, training-free canonicalized averaging is nearly optimal among methods that only weight views, and a 13\% accuracy gain is available to \emph{any} model that adds a reference-supervised residual---including generic regressors fifty times larger than ours, which also produce more self-consistent shapes. The case for a small gated model is therefore not peak accuracy. It is that the same clean accuracy comes with tolerance to a noisy camera, per-view weights that can be inspected, a calibrated per-joint confidence, less sensitivity to the adaptation budget on a new rig, and a cost of 28K parameters. The same results carry two warnings: without the reference repair of Sec.~\ref{sec:reference}, we would have reported a fictitious learning gain built on reference-scale error, and without the collapse analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:collapse} we would have kept attributing meaning to a gate that mathematically cannot deviate from uniform. A third warning is practical: in an earlier iteration of this study, comparing rows trained with different budgets made an 8K-parameter variant look better than the full model by an amount that vanished once the budgets were equalized.

Limitations remain. On Procrustes-aligned error the proposed model is behind whole-skeleton regressors by 2.6\,mm; combining explicit view gating with a whole-skeleton residual is an obvious next design, with the caveat that raw-coordinate residuals transferred worst in our experiments. No learned residual model transfers zero-shot across rigs; deployment on a new vehicle requires re-running the label-free adaptation. The in-cabin reference is triangulated from the same 2D detections that feed the fused streams; \da{}'s independent OpenPose-based reference partially fills this gap, but a physically independent sensor would strengthen absolute claims. Hands still dominate the error budget (202\,mm vs.\ 48\,mm for the non-hand joints) and are at times invisible to all three cameras---a sensing limit that no fusion can overcome. Gaussian corruption is only partially detected by the gate. Head pitch from the fused keypoints is not validated. The scale anchor inherits any global bias of the monocular estimator's body prior (PA-MPJPE is unaffected; one physical baseline measurement would externalize the scale). Finally, our data comes from a driving simulator; validating the self-calibration procedure on moving vehicles with vibration is future work.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a self-calibrated pipeline for multi-view driver pose estimation at the keypoint level: a per-sequence extrinsic refinement with anatomically anchored scale that turns nominal-extrinsics triangulation into a physically consistent reference (3.6\,px mean reprojection error; $0.368\pm0.015$\,m shoulder width; $4.7\times$ more usable front-view observations), and \method{}, a 28K-parameter set-attention fusion network with corruption-aware gating, reference-supervised residual refinement, and calibrated uncertainty. Under one training budget for all methods, the system improves MPJPE by 13.1\% over strong training-free fusion with gains in all four illumination conditions, matches the MPJPE of regressors with fifty times more parameters while remaining robust to a noisy camera where they are not, yields head-yaw measurements of correct amplitude on all 88 drives, and adapts to a new rig within minutes of label-free training supervised only by the rig's own triangulated observations (20.8\,mm PA-MPJPE on \da{}). It does not transfer zero-shot, and it is behind whole-skeleton regressors on Procrustes-aligned error; we state both plainly. The accompanying analyses---consensus self-supervision provably collapses view gating, and reference-scale errors manufacture illusory gains---are cautionary results we believe generalize well beyond driver monitoring.
